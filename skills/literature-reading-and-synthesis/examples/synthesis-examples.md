# Comparing studies without forcing agreement

The following reports are fictional. All use the same stated epithelial-cell model, ligand dose, vehicle comparator and two-hour endpoint; independently initiated cultures are biological replication units. Sample counts and assay details are unspecified. Fold change compares ligand with vehicle; 1 is no change.

| Study | Outcome | Fold change | Reported 95% interval |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A | Marker mRNA | 1.4 | 1.2–1.6 |
| B | Marker protein abundance | 1.0 | 0.9–1.1 |
| C | Marker mRNA | 1.3 | 0.9–1.7 |

Completed synthesis:

> A supports increased mRNA at two hours. C estimates a similar increase but is less precise and also allows no change; it does not establish contradiction or confirmed replication. B measures protein abundance, so its near-no-change estimate does not refute the mRNA finding. These reports support the mRNA response more clearly than a protein response at this endpoint. A later protein response is a possible explanation, not a tested finding.

If B instead measures mRNA with fold change 1.0 and interval 0.95–1.05, the measured questions now match and the reported magnitudes are in tension. Check unreported conditions and a direct comparison appropriate to the question. Protein timing cannot explain a disagreement between two mRNA measurements; separate interval positions alone do not establish a statistical difference.

## Several papers, one underlying dataset

Fictional input: an original cohort paper reports an association, two later papers reanalyze the same cohort and a review cites all three.

> These accounts provide several analyses of one cohort, rather than four independent confirmations. Compare their adjustments and estimands to learn which assumptions affect the association. The review maps those findings but adds no new cohort observations. Causal interpretation and generalization to other populations remain separate questions.

The relevant unit is the evidence contribution, not the number of publication records.

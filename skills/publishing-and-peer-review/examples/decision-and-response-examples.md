# Publication decisions and completed replies

These fictional cases supply closed facts. Their completed written outputs do not document experiments, journal decisions or edits in a user's project.

## A transfer offer after venue-only rejection

**Given:** The paper validates a microbial assay for nutrient-dependent growth responses in the tested culture conditions. The decision says this technically sound paper is too specialized for the journal's audience. No scientific flaw is identified. The letter links a list of possible destinations but reports no consultation with a receiving editor. The authors prefer an affordable microbiology venue; its current fees and sharing terms have not been checked. They ask whether transfer requires new experiments.

**Completed decision:** The stated reason supports choosing a more suitable audience. It does not establish a need for new experiments. Compare the suggested and preferred venues using scope, article type, costs and rights; check unresolved terms before committing. The transfer list offers a route, not evidence of editorial interest or acceptance. If choosing transfer, inspect which files and prior correspondence move and replace the original cover letter. For direct submission, follow its disclosure instructions. Neither route has been executed.

**Draft contribution paragraph:**

> We submit our research article on a microbial assay for distinguishing nutrient-dependent growth responses. The manuscript reports a validated assay and its application to the tested culture conditions. Its contribution is a practical measurement approach for microbiologists studying nutrient-dependent growth; we believe this fits the journal's methods and experimental-microbiology audience.

Required declarations, author approval and destination details remain to be supplied. The paragraph does not claim a transfer endorsement or promise publication.

## Cost does not decide whether a request is necessary

**Given:** A cell-culture manuscript originally claims that compound K improves organism-level disease outcomes. Only a cellular response was tested. Treated and vehicle-control cultures received the same solvent concentration and were processed in parallel. Reviewer 2 requests an animal cohort, an existing-control explanation and clearer wording. The authors have decided to remove the organism-level claim. They completed only these text edits: the abstract's final sentence states the cellular finding; Discussion paragraph 4 limits inference to cultured cells; Methods subsection “Controls” explains the matched vehicle control already used. No new experiment or reanalysis occurred.

**Completed reply, Reviewer 2 point 1:**

> We agree that the cellular measurements do not establish organism-level benefit. We have removed that claim from the abstract's final sentence and revised Discussion paragraph 4 to state that the study addresses the response of cultured cells to compound K. We have not performed an animal experiment. The revised Discussion explicitly states that organism-level outcomes remain untested. The new cohort would be needed to retain the original claim; this revision instead limits the conclusion to the available evidence.

**Completed reply, Reviewer 2 point 2:**

> The original experiment included a matched vehicle control. We have clarified this in Methods, subsection “Controls”: treated and vehicle-control cultures received the same solvent concentration and were processed in parallel. This is a reporting correction describing the existing design; no new control experiment or reanalysis was performed.

The cohort is declined because the revised claim no longer asserts the untested outcome. Expense does not make the original broad claim defensible. If the cellular claim also lacked a necessary control, a limitation sentence alone would not repair it.

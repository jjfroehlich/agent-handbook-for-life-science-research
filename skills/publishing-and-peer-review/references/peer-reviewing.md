# Peer Reviewing

Use this when the user is deciding whether to accept a review invitation, reading a manuscript as a reviewer, drafting a peer-review report, or revising reviewer comments for constructiveness and scope. For a bounded comment edit, inspect the needed evidence and preserve the requested scope rather than require a complete review first.

## The job of this topic

Make peer review useful to the editor and authors. A good review is fair, prioritized, evidence-linked, actionable, timely, and bounded to the manuscript's claims.

## Default workflow

1. Decide whether to accept: expertise, conflict, deadline, access to material, and ability to write a respectful review.
2. Read for the manuscript's question, central claim, contribution and applicable journal criteria.
3. Assess the relevant complete manuscript, figures and supporting material in the order useful for its argument; no fixed number of reading passes is required.
4. Sort concerns by consequence for the inference. A missing label, method detail or citation can be major if it changes interpretation; an extensive optional experiment remains optional.
5. Draft a fair summary before criticism so the editor and authors can see what you understood.
6. Write each major comment as concern, evidence, consequence, and bounded requested action.
7. For statistical concerns, check direct comparisons, experimental units, independence, uncertainty, circularity, exploratory framing, and multiplicity before asking for more analysis.
8. Put confidential recommendations, conflicts, or ethics concerns in the editor-only channel when journal policy supports that split.
9. Final-check tone, feasibility, and whether the review would still be acceptable if signed or published.

## Decision rules

- Disclose expertise gaps to the editor and establish whether a bounded review is useful. Decline when you cannot assess the assigned aspects competently, a conflict cannot be resolved, or the deadline cannot be met; suggest alternatives when useful.
- If the paper is weak but salvageable, focus on the few validity or interpretation changes that would most improve it.
- If the concern is only style or preference, keep it minor and avoid making it a condition of publication.
- If an experiment would become a new project, ask whether the claim can instead be narrowed, caveated, clarified, or tested with existing data.
- If statistics are outside your competence, flag the concern and suggest statistical review rather than inventing objections.
- If the journal asks for confidential comments, use them for editor-only synthesis or sensitive issues, not for comments authors need to answer.
- If reviewing a revision, evaluate whether required changes were addressed and avoid adding unrelated new demands unless a new decisive issue appears.

## Check the evidence that could change the judgment

Connect reported quantities to methods, labels and legends instead of taking the display at face value. For an associated dataset, screen whether it is accessible or has explained restrictions, whether names/content/formats are understandable, whether files match the manuscript, whether the arrangement is usable, and whether values are physically plausible and internally consistent. These are issue screens, not proof of reproducibility or a demand for unrestricted confidential data. Declare what you could not verify.

For image-based evidence, inspect whether processing preserves information and whether different fields, gels, exposures, composites and nonlinear changes are disclosed. Selective enhancement, erasure or concealment can change the evidence. Check available originals and current journal requirements; do not infer intent or conduct a private investigation from a display anomaly.

When a biological inference depends on a technique, choose the relevant diagnostic rather than demand more methods:

- A perturbation can alter interacting processes, and compensation can mask an effect. A loss phenotype does not alone identify a direct regulatory role.
- Detection limits and label interference can change what an image reveals. A proxy must represent the target and avoid perturbing it; unexpected proxy behavior may require investigation rather than literal interpretation.
- Isolation can change a property by removing its context. Separately, ask which entities or states an assay can distinguish: a precise combined signal cannot identify a change in one component when the assay conflates them.
- Reconstitution may recover one property without recreating the complete system. A simulation's inference depends on included assumptions and omitted features.

One well-used approach can support a claim; several poorly used approaches need not. Additional evidence should distinguish a live alternative or address a consequential limitation. Reproducible measurements and a persuasive account do not by themselves establish a unique interpretation. When useful, ask authors to explain the prior model, the proposed change and its evidential basis in plain language; this is not a mandatory new Methods section.

### Statistical concerns

Start with the scientific comparison and independent unit, including repeated or nested measurements. A change after treatment alone cannot separate intervention from time or practice. Separate significant and nonsignificant tests do not establish different effects: the between-effect contrast needs its own estimate and uncertainty. Degrees of freedom alone cannot diagnose an incorrectly specified mixed model.

For associations, inspect influential observations and group structure. Determine whether an extreme value is a documented error or a genuine observation before correcting or excluding it. Pooled and within-group associations can answer different questions. Select the estimand and a suitable model or sensitivity analysis; rank correlations, robust labels and bootstrapping are not assumption-free cures.

Check whether data-dependent selection biases the same test toward the claimed effect. A selected subset can be valid when selection and testing are appropriately independent under the null; a data split must respect dependence. Distinguish planned analyses from useful exploration and a later prespecified replication. Preregistration cannot retrospectively make a discovery confirmatory. Ask for relevant decision history without inferring hidden intent.

For multiple comparisons, identify the actual family and inferential goal. A large prespecified family can still need error control; do not demand correction for every hypothetical test that might have been run. A correction label is insufficient if the family or assumptions are wrong.

Judge sample adequacy by design, meaningful effect sizes, precision and the feasible population. No fixed sample-count threshold establishes validity, and post-hoc power from the observed estimate is not independent confirmation. Selected significant small-sample estimates can exaggerate magnitude. Nonsignificance may be imprecision rather than evidence of negligible effect; equivalence needs a meaningful margin and suitable analysis, while Bayesian evidence depends on specified models and priors. Avoid treating nearby p-values on opposite sides of a threshold as categorically different biology. A p-value is not the probability the claim is true, its importance or the actual error rate of this result.

Association alone does not identify causation. Consider reverse direction, common causes and the actual design assumptions. Manipulation can leave confounding, and mediation or adding a covariate is not by itself causal identification. Explain the specific inference limit, narrow the claim or request specialist input rather than impose a universal test-selection recipe.

## Make requests and recommendations proportionate

First explain the concern; then allow a scientifically adequate answer. Clarifying an already appropriate method, reanalysis, new evidence and a narrower claim are different options, not interchangeable repairs. An invalid design cannot be rescued by a polite explanation. Authors retain presentation choices when the work is sound and clear; useful future experiments can remain suggestions.

Practitioner advice differs about demanding experiments, predicting impact and recommending rejection. Use the actual journal remit and the present evidence rather than adopt a categorical rule from either side. Screening does not guarantee suitability. Explain an unsalvageable defect without inventing a feasible repair; request revision only when resolving the specified concerns could change your judgment. A sound paper can receive acceptance with discretionary suggestions.

## Variants and edge cases

- **Very poor manuscript:** Still write a professional review. State the main validity or fit barriers without sarcasm or pile-on details.
- **Likely accept:** Explain why the evidence supports the claims and identify residual issues only when present. Do not manufacture criticism to make the review look substantial.
- **Revision review:** Compare the revised manuscript against the prior decision letter and major unresolved issues; do not restart review from scratch unless the journal asks.
- **Portable or transferred review:** Reconsider comments in the context of the new journal's criteria and any changed manuscript.
- **Statistical paper or heavy analysis:** Check direct comparisons, independence, assumptions, uncertainty, multiplicity, and whether claims match the analysis.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting reviews despite conflicts, missing expertise, or impossible timelines.
- Writing a review as a list of reactions with no priority.
- Demanding citations, analyses, or experiments that mainly serve reviewer preference.
- Using anonymity to be dismissive, sarcastic, or personal.
- Telling authors the accept/reject recommendation when the journal expects that to remain confidential.
- Copyediting before assessing claims, methods, and evidence.

## Diagnostic questions

- Can I review this competently and on time?
- Can I summarize the manuscript's main claim in one or two sentences?
- Which issues would change the editor's decision?
- Are my major requests necessary for the current claim, or am I expanding the project?
- Does each criticism include evidence and its consequence? Offer a remedy where one exists; explain an unsalvageable flaw rather than inventing a feasible revision.
- Are statistical comparisons direct and are units of analysis independent?
- Would I stand behind this wording if the review were signed or published?

## Output patterns / mini-templates

**Review report skeleton**

```text
Summary
[Neutral summary of question, approach, and central claim.]

Major comments
1. Concern: [issue]
   Evidence: [where it appears]
   Consequence: [why it affects validity, interpretation, or fit]
   Requested action: [bounded revision, analysis, claim limit, or clarification]

Minor comments
- [clarity, figure, terminology, or presentation issue]

Confidential comments to editor, if needed
[Decision-relevant synthesis, conflict disclosure, ethics concern, or recommendation according to journal policy.]
```

**Constructive major comment pattern**

```text
The manuscript claims [claim], but [evidence/method/detail] does not yet support that scope. A bounded way to address this would be to [specific action], or to narrow the claim to [supported version].
```

**Statistical concern pattern**

```text
The comparison appears to test each condition separately rather than directly comparing the conditions. Please clarify the analysis strategy or provide a direct comparison aligned with the stated claim.
```

## Examples

- When only a cellular response was measured, replace "The authors need many more animal experiments" with "The current data support the measured cellular response but not the organism-level claim. Please either add evidence that directly supports that claim or narrow the conclusion to the tested system."
- Instead of "This is badly written," identify the missing explanation: "I could not tell from the opening how the stated question relates to the comparison in Figure 2. Please explain that connection where the question is introduced." Use this only if the manuscript actually has that problem.

## When not to apply this

Do not use this playbook for author response letters except to understand what a reviewer comment is asking for. Use the response-to-reviewers playbook for author-facing replies.

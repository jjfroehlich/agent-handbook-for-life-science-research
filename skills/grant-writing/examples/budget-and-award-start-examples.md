# Budget and award-start examples

These closed fictional cases illustrate decisions. They are not current funder rules or evidence about the user's project.

## Choosing a feasible pilot

Given: a nine-month method-development opportunity allows a total request up to €35,000. Institutional finance staff have costed the proposed pilot at €29,000, including €3,000 institutional costs within that total. The current call allows its service charges. Eligibility and institutional submission have been checked. Equipment and space are supported by the institution; the facility quote and capacity are conditional on award. There are no pilot measurements. A larger two-year opportunity allows €180,000 but would need unresolved seasonal access and a workflow that has not been tested. The user asks which application to prepare first.

Response: “Prepare the pilot first if its development objective fits your priority. Its €29,000 total is within the €35,000 cap, and the checked cost categories support the proposed scope. Confirm the conditional facility quote and capacity at the relevant stage. The larger award remains an option, but its seasonal access and workflow dependencies need resolution before promising the broader work. The pilot application should explain how workflow performance will be assessed; it cannot cite preliminary measurements that do not exist.”

The larger cap does not establish greater feasibility. Neither option authorizes spending.

## Selection with delayed facility access

Given: an applicant has received a selection notice for a paired freshwater-sample preservation project. Award preparation continues and the final start arrangements are not confirmed. Core-facility access will be eight weeks later than planned. The institution has already authorized resources for protocol and analysis preparation. No preservation comparison has been run. The user asks for a short handoff note.

Note: “Hold sample processing and purchases that depend on the facility and unconfirmed start arrangements. The applicant and grants officer should seek clarification of the revised access date and any award implications through the institution's process. Meanwhile, use the already authorized resources to prepare protocols and the analysis plan. Confirm facility timing, applicable start and cost conditions, and technical readiness before beginning the dependent comparison.”

The note does not turn selection into spending authority, claim that either preservation method is better, or substitute an assay simply because it is available. It proposes coordination; no communication or commitment has been performed.

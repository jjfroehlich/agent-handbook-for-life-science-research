# Budget And Feasibility

## Use this when...

Use this when the proposal needs stronger budget logic, preliminary data selection, feasibility evidence, risk plans, applicant-capacity evidence, or project-deliverability review.

## The job of this topic

Show reviewers that the proposed work can be executed with the requested resources, available evidence, applicant capacity, and credible alternatives.

## Default workflow

1. List promised work packages and the resources each requires.
2. Check personnel, equipment, travel, services, data, institutional support, and funder-allowed costs.
3. Select preliminary data because it answers reviewer feasibility concerns, not because it shows general productivity.
4. Distinguish uncertainty about the scientific answer from uncertainty about whether the proposed work can be performed. For planned development, identify qualification milestones, resources, and decisions if they are not met.
5. Use relevant CV, publication, engagement, independence, environment, or team evidence to support execution capacity; experience with a model does not establish that an untested assay works.
6. Separate completed work from the future work requested for funding. A completed pilot can motivate an extension, but label the pilot and the remaining work accurately and check the scheme's eligibility rules.

## Decision rules

- If the budget cannot deliver the promised work, rescope the project or revise the costs.
- If preliminary data do not support a feasibility claim, replace or reorganize them.
- If execution depends on unavailable data, undeveloped methods, or uncertain access, assess whether developing that capability is part of the funded purpose. Specify evidence, an access or development plan, qualification criteria, and a credible scope decision; an unexplained promise is insufficient.
- If work is already complete, do not present it as proposed future work.

## Variants and edge cases

- Fellowships often need applicant, lab, mentor, and environment evidence.
- Early-career grants may need independence evidence without turning the application into a CV prize.
- Empirical projects may need access, representativeness, mergeability, and identification strategy.
- Some project problems after award should be handled through funder or research-office communication rather than silence.

## Anti-patterns

- Under-budgeting to look efficient while making the project undeliverable.
- Showing random preliminary data as a productivity record.
- Hiding missing data access or unplanned method development behind "high risk" language.
- Packaging completed or circulating work as future funded work.

## Diagnostic questions

- What reviewer concern does each preliminary data item reduce?
- Which work package is least deliverable with the requested resources?
- Which costs are disallowed or under-supported?
- What evidence supports the applicant or team's capacity, and which capabilities remain to be established?
- What is the fallback if the main method, data source, or recruitment path fails?

## Examples

Weak: "Preliminary data show the lab has worked on this topic."

If those measurements exist, identify the tested conditions and resolution and explain the feasibility concern they address. If they do not, write: "The team has experience with the model; the proposed assay's required resolution remains to be established during calibration." Do not replace missing evidence with a confident sentence.

## When not to apply this

Do not provide institutional finance or legal advice. Flag budget-rule uncertainty and tell the user what must be checked with the funder or institution.

## Protocol Completeness And Budget Calibration

For a whole-plan or feasibility review, audit whether the relevant sections explain:

- Hypotheses and goals.
- Controls and comparison groups.
- Methods, analyses, and success criteria.
- Site, system, population, or model rationale.
- Relevant references and excluded-but-expected methods.
- Personnel, services, equipment, travel, and timeline dependencies.

For foundation-style budgets, use visible prior awards or public giving records as context when available, not as a substitute for the current call or actual delivery costs. Justify each expense. If an LOI preceded the proposal, explain any scope or amount change and check the applicable process before submission.

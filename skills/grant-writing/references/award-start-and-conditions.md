# Award conditions and readiness to begin

## Use this when...

Use this for an application's transition from selection to a planned project handoff, or a draft explanation of an award-related dependency. General grant administration, legal decisions, spending, and contact with funders require their own authorized context.

## Default workflow

1. Identify what is actually known: selection notice, agreement status, authorized start and costs, institutional approvals, and readiness of the proposed methods and resources. Selection alone does not establish the other facts.
2. Read the applicable current award documents with the responsible grants, finance, legal, or research-office staff where necessary. Award form, eligible costs, signatories, reporting, partner obligations, and amendment rules differ; historical advice is not the governing document.
3. For a planned handoff, identify owners of scientific work and required management tasks. Where partners must compare or combine outputs, specify compatible methods, endpoints, data formats, and responsibilities.
4. Plan records from the start and work backward from actual reporting dates. Include communication, dissemination, data, or other plans only where the award requires them; a proportionate project meeting can settle responsibilities.
5. If access, timing, partner capacity, or another consequential condition changes, identify the affected work and the institution's route for clarification or approval before promising a replacement or commitment.

## Decision rules

- If the agreement or spending authority is unresolved, keep it unresolved in the advice. Separate permissible preparation from work or purchases that depend on confirmation.
- If finance and approvals are in place but an assay is unqualified, identify the technical qualification still needed. Administrative permission does not validate a method.
- If a proposed change could affect contractual outputs, dates, costs, or partner commitments, check the applicable change procedure with the responsible institution. Do not assume every routine adjustment needs an amendment or that a significant change is automatically allowed.
- Explain the consequences for scope, timing, costs, partners and outputs before seeking a change. A major redesign may undermine the award's basis or be impermissible; supportive officer advice is not an approved amendment or permission to redesign during agreement preparation.
- For EU or other collaborative awards, adapt these questions to the actual agreement and institutional arrangements. Do not infer present rules, rates, portals, or deadlines from an advice article.

## Distinguish scientific and institutional authority

Identify the actual beneficiaries and authorized signatories rather than assume the scientific leader can sign. Scientific, administrative and financial coordination can be held by different people; delegation does not remove the relevant coordinator or institutional accountability. Size competent grants/finance/legal support to the award, and agree appropriate officer contact and prior-approval questions rather than require a project manager for every fellowship.

Read beyond the scientific annex. Reconcile who does what, effort, dates, deliverables, milestones, risks and relevant ethics/security with the actual agreement. Actual-cost, unit-cost and lump-sum forms can carry different financial/reporting conditions; use the applicable documents and expertise instead of extrapolating from a previous award. Preserve the selected proposal's content except properly requested or authorized clarification. For sponsored work, expose publication or thesis restrictions to affected trainees and the responsible office before commitments; a signature or scientific ambition does not remove them.

## Plan evidence and reporting at the handoff

Assign owners and actual dates for required kick-off/reviews, secure records, deliverables, data stewardship and communication/dissemination/exploitation work. Scale the meeting and records to the project; some outputs remain confidential. Use actual retention terms rather than a universal five-year rule. Where required, plan an updated data-management plan and the applicable acknowledgment/disclaimer; FAIR does not mean unrestricted access to sensitive data.

Distinguish continuous outputs from periodic progress/cost reports, partner contributions and any approval/payment dependency. Submitting a report alone is not clearance or guaranteed payment. A manager can help collect information without replacing accountability. A permitted later publication update may be courteous but is not an extra reporting duty or a proven funding advantage.

## Set usable partner arrangements

For collaborative work, agree reciprocal contributions, financial responsibilities, decisions, report writers, supervision and communication. Check that methods/endpoints allow the required comparison or combination without forcing unlike systems into identical procedures. A funder agreement and an internal partner agreement settle different relationships; the archived EU example gives the former precedence and excludes the funder from signing the latter, but actual scheme rules govern.

Adapt questions about data/material/notebook access after completion, credit criteria/process, institutional attribution, public contacts, IP timing, conflicts and redirection/departure to the real project. Check restrictions on use, onward transfer, derivatives and publication with the authorized institution. A template or email is neither automatic legal sufficiency nor reciprocal understanding, implemented support or actual access. Revisit contributions without promising fixed byline rights or that others will finish a departed person's work. Ordinary coordination cannot require unsafe mediation or conceal serious misconduct.

## Useful output

For a bounded question, return the dependency, what it prevents, what remains possible, and the document or responsible role needed to resolve it. Use an owner-and-date table only when coordinating several tasks would help. Do not submit, contact anyone, sign, purchase, or commit resources merely because you have drafted a plan.

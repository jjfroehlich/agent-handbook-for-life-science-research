# Paper Structure

Use this when the user asks for whole-manuscript flow, section order, title or heading logic, paragraph organization, or a structural diagnosis before rewriting.

## The job of this topic

Paper structure turns a set of analyses into a readable argument. The reader should be able to identify the central contribution, see why each section exists, and follow the movement from motivation to evidence to interpretation.

## Default workflow

1. State the one main contribution or organizing question; if there are several, name the bridge that makes them one paper, chapter, or review.
2. Identify the section jobs under the actual article format: abstract summarizes, introduction motivates, methods make the work assessable, results present findings with needed local explanation, discussion synthesizes, and figures/legends communicate their evidence. Combined sections and methods/resource papers may organize these jobs differently.
3. Build the evidence spine: each major result should answer a subquestion or test a prediction that advances the central contribution.
4. Check whether the current order explains the scientific logic. Chronology can help when order or time is itself consequential.
5. Audit headings, topic sentences, figure titles, and panel order for argumentative progress; convert method labels to result-bearing labels when evidence supports that.
6. Use context-content-conclusion where it clarifies the argument: orient the reader, deliver new content and make the inference recoverable. Do not require all three moves explicitly in every paragraph or repeat a conclusion the passage already makes clear.
7. Check paragraph mechanics: topic sentence, support and necessary detail. Add a closing inference or transition when the reasoning needs one. Split competing lines of reasoning; keep related moves together when they explain one point.
8. Relocate details that interrupt the argument while keeping claim-essential evidence and contrary findings accessible. Length pressure cannot justify hiding support needed to assess the main conclusion.
9. For an unsettled full draft, resolve structural problems before extensive line editing. For a requested local edit, preserve settled structure and improve the relevant sentences by recovering base clauses, unpacking noun stacks and making parallel evidence grammatically parallel.

## Decision rules

- If the paper is a list of analyses, group them under one question and order them by the logic of the claim.
- If a paragraph switches between unrelated points or misplaced section functions, split it or move the misplaced material; retain connected question, evidence and interpretation when they develop one point.
- If research chronology obscures the argument, reorder around actual questions, predictions or claims without inventing dependencies or presenting exploratory work as prespecified.
- If headings are method labels, consider result-bearing headings unless that would overclaim or violate venue norms.
- If multiple related findings use different syntax, use parallel form so the reader sees conceptual differences rather than grammatical noise.
- If the introduction or discussion contains detailed methods, move the reproducibility detail to methods and keep only orientation.
- If sentence-level edits make the prose smoother but leave the reader confused about the claim, prioritize structural revision.
- If a style rule would cut necessary precision, keep the precision and improve placement, order, or base-clause clarity instead.
- If the title states a topic rather than a result, test whether a result-stating title is accurate, short, and supported.
- If a figure or table has no clear role in the argument, revise its purpose or move it to supporting material. Descriptive resources, workflows and summaries can have a main-text role without a finding-stating title.
- If the paper has a writing-process problem, separate drafting order from final reader order; it is acceptable to draft results first while presenting context first.
- If coauthor sections feel stitched together, a shared outline with short paragraph jobs can expose incompatible scopes before line editing. Preserve a coherent existing structure when only a local edit is requested.

## Sentence choices that preserve meaning

Recover the base clause and identify what each modifier attaches to before cutting words. Check actor, action, referent, quantity, units, comparator, time, negation and uncertainty. A grammatically removable qualification may be scientifically essential. Keep a stable name for the same entity; replacing it with varied synonyms can imply a different entity. A proxy measurement is not identical to the biological process it represents.

Prefer concrete verbs when the actual action is known. Nominalizations, compound terms, prepositions and negatives can still serve useful purposes; do not use deletion lists as rules. For example, temporal dynamics may be the quantity under study. Never replace “not significantly different” with “equivalent,” fill an absent magnitude, or invent an actor to make a sentence active.

Choose active or passive voice according to the needed focus. Active can clarify agency; passive can keep the measured entity as the topic. Use parallel grammar when the comparisons really are parallel. Short sentences can orient or emphasize, while a controlled longer sentence can preserve a condition and its consequence together. Tense follows whether a procedure is planned/completed, a finding is being reported or a current inference is stated. Punctuation should reveal grammatical relationships, not merely where the writer pauses.

Keep the voice appropriate to the audience and genre. Conversational clarity requires selecting and organizing thoughts, not transcribing rambling speech or imitating another writer. Humor, drama or metaphor should help the reader. An anthropomorphic explanation must not assert biological intention; use it only with a clear boundary and an accurate scientific explanation, or retain it as a provisional intuition rather than a mechanism. Do not invent a precise translation to rescue a metaphor.

More engaging wording is not necessarily stronger evidence. Observational associations between abstract language and citations do not show that rewriting causes more citations or improves comprehension. Test-reader feedback can expose an unclear passage, but enthusiasm, agreement or fluent prose alone does not validate its science. Distinguish a misunderstanding from a substantive scientific disagreement before choosing a repair.

When editing quotations or attributed summaries, preserve the source's meaning and attribution. Do not silently rewrite a quotation as though the source used the revised words. Keep the author's position distinguishable from the manuscript's own critique; shortening must not remove a qualification that changes the position being represented.

## Writing Methods that match the work

Connect each reported finding to the actual design, materials, consequential parameters, processing and analysis. Include applicable sampling, exclusions, versions, ethics and data records from supplied documentation; never fill a template with invented settings, controls or approvals. Explain why important choices were made and what limits they introduce without retrospectively constructing an ideal rationale.

Describe shared procedures once when that reduces repetition, while identifying the affected results and modifications to cited methods. Retain detail needed to recover the work; routine loading or rearrangement need not become a catalogue when it changes no relevant result. A table or schematic can clarify an inventory. Where inference depends on a prior model, explain that model and the reasoning linking the observations to its proposed revision; this is an option for clarity, not a mandatory journal section.

Placement follows the actual architecture: a common Methods section, methods within each study, or an integrated development/results account may fit different work. Tense should distinguish planned and completed procedures from current theoretical statements. Contemporaneous notes and an informed reader can reveal omissions; a clear account alone does not prove successful reproduction.

## Article and drafting variants

- Review articles may organize around a conceptual question rather than one new result.
- Tool or resource papers should make the use case and validation logic visible early.
- Thesis chapters may require broader field framing and institution-specific structure.
- Multi-author manuscripts often need a stronger shared outline because sections may otherwise reflect different local priorities.
- Methods, tool, or resource papers should expose use case and validation logic early, not only biological downstream observations.
- When the user's problem is getting started, use storyboards, figure-first drafting, timed drafting, or coauthor checkpoints as process scaffolds, then return to section and evidence checks.

Use these scaffolds for the actual bottleneck. A provisional abstract can clarify an early plan and be revised after the findings are settled; it need not wait until every other section exists. Plan completion around real dependencies, uncertainty and alternative routes, not hoped-for data presented as obtained results. For a collaborative full paper, prepare permitted data/metadata records early and keep a suitable shared version history. A fixed timer, page quota or prolonged persistence is not a general requirement. A locally chosen trainee-first drafting exercise serves a learning purpose; it does not override a settled request for expert, collaborative or accessibility-related drafting help.

When assistance intersects with an explicit learning goal, distinguish the particular task the user can evaluate from the reasoning they are trying to learn. The same person may appropriately automate a familiar formatting step while practicing an unfamiliar scientific argument with feedback. Agree on help that serves the stated goal; do not classify the person permanently as novice or expert, impose a general AI ban, or override authorized assistance merely because they are a trainee.

For review prose, first identify whether the requested artifact is descriptive or integrative. An integrative review needs relationships, disagreements, methodological limits and an actual added perspective; a descriptive review can present selected work separately. Keep source-linked notes and copied wording distinguishable before rewriting. Treat favored and contrary work fairly, and state the supplied coverage and time limits honestly. A review label does not establish an exhaustive search, current completeness or valid quantitative pooling. Consult literature-reading guidance if new evidence synthesis is needed; do not automatically launch a search for a prose-only revision.

## Anti-patterns

- Chronology that obscures the question or inference.
- Every result presented with equal weight.
- Important findings buried at the end of paragraphs.
- Vague topic sentences such as "We next examined..." when a result statement is possible.
- Mechanical style edits that remove nuance or change scientific meaning.
- Vague verbs such as studied, investigated, modulated, or assessed when the actual action, direction, or comparison can be named.
- Treating a productivity tactic as a substitute for deciding the claim, evidence spine, or section jobs.

## Diagnostic questions

- What is the one sentence version of the paper's contribution?
- What explanatory job does each section serve, and where is brief overlap useful?
- Can the reader scan headings, topic sentences, and figure titles and recover the story?
- Which figure or table carries each major claim?
- Does each paragraph develop a coherent point, with connected moves and a recoverable inference where needed?
- Which details are necessary in the main text, and which belong elsewhere?
- Where does the draft over-explain, under-explain, or repeat itself?
- Which sentence edits risk changing uncertainty, comparison, or scope?

## Output patterns / mini-templates

- Structural diagnosis: `Main claim: ... Reader blockage: ... Section-level fix: ... Sentence-level fix after structure: ...`
- Paragraph job labels: `Context -> gap -> result -> evidence -> interpretation -> limitation -> transition.`
- Results unit: `Context -> approach -> result -> detail/robustness -> local conclusion.`
- Heading revision: `Method label: "Analysis of X" -> result-bearing label: "X increases under Y condition"` when evidence supports it.
- Sentence repair: recover the base clause first, then add only modifiers needed for scientific accuracy.
- Evidence spine: `Question -> Figure/table -> finding -> support -> local conclusion -> next question.`
- Manuscript triage: `Keep in main text / move to methods / move to supplement / cut / reserve for discussion.`
- Hypothesis-linked outline: `Question -> prediction -> comparison -> result -> figure/table -> interpretation boundary.`
- Sentence repair pass: `Base clause -> necessary modifiers -> comparison/quantity -> caveat -> rhythm.`
- Coauthor alignment note: `Section job: ... Required claim: ... Required evidence: ... Details to move: ...`

## Examples

Illustrative fixed facts for the first three pairs: the question is whether treatment changes cell-state composition; single-cell sequencing compares treated and control samples and shows an expanded inflammatory population, which motivates subsequent cytokine-response gene analysis. Independent assays A and B consistently show increased X under condition Y; assay A establishes direction and assay B localizes it to that cell state. These are fictional supplied inputs, not findings recovered from the sparse before sentences.

Before: `We performed assay A. We then performed assay B. Finally, we analyzed the data.`

After: `Condition Y consistently increased X across two independent assays. Assay A showed the direction of the effect, and assay B localized it to the relevant cell state.`

Before: `Analysis of cell states`

After: `Treatment enriches the inflammatory cell state`

Why it works: the revised heading tells readers what claim the section will support rather than naming only the analysis type.

Before: `First, samples were collected. Then we ran sequencing. Then we compared the groups.`

After: `To test whether treatment shifted cell-state composition, we profiled control and treated samples by single-cell sequencing. Treated samples showed an increased inflammatory population, motivating a focused comparison of cytokine-response genes.`

Before: `The measurement of differences in the expression values between the treatment and control conditions was carried out for the analysis of pathway activation.`

After: `We compared expression values between treatment and control samples.`

Why it works: the revised sentence restores actor, action and comparison. The original names a pathway-activation purpose but does not supply how expression measures activation. Explain that relationship if supported; do not silently turn the expression comparison into an activation estimate.

When no findings are supplied:

Before: `We performed assay A. We then performed assay B. Finally, we analyzed the data.`

After: `We analyzed data from assays A and B.`

Needed for a claim-bearing revision: the tested question, the role of each assay and the observed finding. Do not infer agreement, direction or cell state from assay chronology.

## When not to apply this

Do not use this playbook as a substitute for venue instructions, journal formatting, or statistical review.

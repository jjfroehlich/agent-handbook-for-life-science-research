# Authorship And Contributions

Use this when the user asks for author contribution statements, acknowledgements, contribution wording, or careful language around authorship-sensitive manuscript text.

## The job of this topic

This topic helps word contribution and acknowledgement text clearly while respecting roles, consent, policy boundaries, and relationship sensitivity.

## Default workflow

1. Separate the artifact type: author contributions, acknowledgements, funding, conflict statement, or dispute-adjacent wording.
2. Identify supplied contributions and avoid inferring unprovided roles.
3. Use contribution language that names concrete work rather than status, seniority, or vague help; prefer controlled role labels when the venue requires them.
4. Keep role descriptions, authorship eligibility/order and acknowledgements distinct. Check possible eligibility before settling acknowledgement wording for substantial design or intellectual revision.
5. Check whether permission is needed for named acknowledgements, because acknowledgement can imply endorsement.
6. Flag policy-sensitive questions and advise checking journal, institution, or group agreements.
7. Protect privacy for people, participants, or groups who should not be identifiable.
8. For dispute-adjacent wording, keep the prose factual, role-based, and non-accusatory.

## Decision rules

- If the user asks who should be an author, do not adjudicate; help map contributions to the relevant policy criteria and suggest a transparent discussion.
- Funding, facilities, administration or language help alone need not establish authorship. Do not exclude someone who also made qualifying contributions, or downgrade substantial design and intellectual revision to thanks before checking the applicable criteria.
- If contribution wording is vague, revise toward concrete verbs such as conceptualized, analyzed, supervised, funded, drafted, reviewed, curated, or performed.
- If AI-assisted tools were used for writing, drafting, data collection, analysis, or figure generation, draft disclosure language in the appropriate section and keep humans responsible for final content.
- If the user wants to list an AI tool as an author, do not do that; provide acknowledgement or methods disclosure wording instead.
- If a dispute is active, keep the language neutral and recommend documenting contributions and consulting appropriate policy channels.
- If naming permission or privacy is unresolved, flag it or use an appropriate group/generic description. First names alone do not establish anonymity.
- If a contribution statement mixes roles and praise, remove praise and keep auditable actions.
- If the venue requires controlled roles, map user-provided facts to controlled role names without adding roles.

## Roles, eligibility and order

Use CRediT labels for activities actually performed. A role can coexist with others; job title, seniority, file type and byline position do not establish what someone did.

| Activity distinction | What the wording should preserve |
|---|---|
| Conceptualization; Methodology; Formal analysis; Investigation | Goals, method/model development, formal analysis of data, and experiments/data collection are different activities. |
| Data curation; Software | Annotation/metadata, cleaning and maintained interpretability differ from programming, algorithms, implementation and code testing; both may apply. |
| Funding acquisition; Resources; Project administration; Supervision | Obtaining finance, supplying materials/tools, coordinating execution and providing oversight/mentorship are distinct contributions. |
| Validation; Visualization | Checking replication/reproducibility differs from presenting data visually. Declaring a validation role does not prove successful reproduction. |
| Writing – original draft; Writing – review & editing | Initial composition, including substantive translation, differs from critical review/commentary/revision, which can occur before or after publication. |

These definitions do not determine eligibility, rank contributions or supply an author-order algorithm. Use the policy applicable to the actual venue and institution. If it uses the four-criterion model, check all four together: substantial conception/design or data acquisition/analysis/interpretation; drafting or important intellectual revision; final approval; and agreement to accountability for investigating/resolving accuracy or integrity questions about any part of the work. Substantial contributors must have a real opportunity to participate in revision and approval; do not exclude them by withholding that opportunity. Opportunity does not itself establish that approval or accountability has occurred.

Include all who actually qualify under the applicable policy. Group authorship still needs clear individual author/collaborator identification and responsibilities. An author need not be expert in every technique. Corresponding contact duties, a named guarantor's whole-work role where applicable, shared authorship accountability and byline order are different questions. Administrative tasks may be delegated without erasing the corresponding contact's communication responsibility or making correspondence a seniority rank. Patent inventorship needs a separate specialist determination.

Older policy quotations may use different criteria. Do not silently combine them, or certify a policy as current from an archived guide. A person's location, service/core label or contribution note alone cannot establish eligibility or justify an accusation of misconduct.

## Revisable credit and unresolved concerns

When order cannot yet be known, agree criteria and a decision process rather than inventing an order or postponing every discussion. Record actual work, provisional responsibilities, changes and unresolved questions. An early plan cannot waive later qualifying work, approval or applicable criteria.

For departures or changing collaborations, identify what happens to data/material access, credit and unfinished writing. Remaining writers and approval contacts can be useful planning questions, but do not assume others have agreed to finish a manuscript or that materials may move. Make hidden coordination and junior contributions visible without conferring authorship merely as an opportunity or reward.

Keep disagreement factual. Separate valid criticism, missing explanation and a mistaken evaluation; respond with evidence or clarification rather than personal attack, invented praise or automatic obedience. Essential support cannot be replaced by a future-work promise. Under the four-criterion model discussed above, eligibility/order are collective author decisions and unresolved eligibility disputes call for institutional investigation rather than journal arbitration. Check the actual policy and authority for a live case; detailed review, correction and appeal procedures belong to the publication workflow.

Where dependence or retaliation risk matters, do not make supervisor permission, advance notice, direct confrontation or mediation prerequisites for seeking appropriate independent advice. This is an editorial boundary on hierarchical advice, not a promise that a particular route is confidential or protective. Check the actual options and do not adjudicate misconduct from an incomplete account.

## Variants and edge cases

- Some journals require a controlled contribution taxonomy; keep wording compatible with that format.
- Thesis acknowledgements can be more personal when local instructions allow it; professional-only rules may constrain them. Professional-first ordering is a default, with a permitted memorial dedication one possible exception.
- Organizing thanks by actual contribution can help; sample recipients, praise and a concluding summary are not requirements.
- Group or facility acknowledgements may be better than naming every individual.
- Funding and institutional support should follow venue requirements rather than improvised prose.
- Large collaborative projects may need group-level contribution language plus individual role detail where required.

## Anti-patterns

- Using acknowledgements to compensate someone who likely meets authorship criteria.
- Listing authorship based on hierarchy without contribution detail.
- Thanking confidential participants in identifiable ways.
- Turning a contribution statement into praise rather than documentation.
- Giving legal or institutional determinations beyond the supplied policy context.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is this authorship, contribution documentation, or acknowledgement?
- Which roles are known from the user-provided facts?
- Does the wording imply responsibility for work the person did not do?
- Does any named person need permission or anonymization?
- Which policy or venue requirement should govern the final wording?
- Is the wording documentation, gratitude, disclosure, or dispute-sensitive communication?

## Output patterns / mini-templates

- Contribution statement: `[Name/initials]: [only the activities actually supplied].` Do not append manuscript review by default.
- Acknowledgement: `We thank [role/person/group] for [specific support], and [facility/funder] for [specific resource].`
- AI-use working note: `[Tool/version, if known] was used for [actual assistance]. Human review performed: [only the supplied checks and reviewers]. Unresolved before release: [required disclosures or approvals not yet established].` Turn this into final disclosure only when the facts and destination requirements are known.
- Dispute-safe note: `Based on the described contributions, document each role and compare them against the journal and institutional authorship criteria before finalizing order or inclusion.`
- Role-mapping note: `Known facts -> possible contribution role -> wording caveat -> policy item to verify.`

## Examples

Read `examples/credit-and-disclosure-examples.md` when an acknowledgement may conceal unresolved eligibility, a role list could imply unperformed checks, or an AI disclosure needs fact-limited wording.

Illustrative fixed facts: A made a substantial experimental-design contribution and substantive intellectual revisions. Naming A is permitted, but final approval and accountability have not yet been discussed. The applicable policy uses all four criteria above.

Before: `We thank A for helping a lot with the project.`

Working record: `A contributed to experimental design and substantive manuscript revision. Final approval and accountability remain to be discussed under the applicable authorship policy.`

Give A a real opportunity to meet the remaining criteria before deciding whether this belongs in author contributions or acknowledgements. Permission to name A does not settle eligibility. If the changed facts instead establish only a permitted equipment loan and no other qualifying work, a specific acknowledgement may be appropriate.

For AI assistance, distinguish use, responsibility and completed review. Human authors retain responsibility under applicable policy, but that duty is not evidence that every author has already reviewed or approved the output. Writing versus analysis/figure uses can require different disclosure locations; check the actual requirements. A tool is not an author under the guidance represented here. Do not assert unsupplied tool use, checking, approval, originality or conflict declarations.

## When not to apply this

Do not settle disputed eligibility/order or legal responsibility from roles or incomplete facts. Provide accurate wording, a policy-to-facts comparison and the consequential unresolved questions; do not send messages or finalize agreements on the user's behalf without authorization.

# Abstracts

Use this when the user is drafting, compressing, diagnosing, or revising an abstract or brief paper summary.

## The job of this topic

An abstract lets readers recognize the contribution and understand the evidence without the full paper. Supply the context, purpose, approach, findings and meaning needed for that task. These jobs can share sentences; an established theme can supply the opening, and a substantive finding can supply the ending. Do not invent a gap or add an impact claim merely to complete a pattern.

## Default workflow

1. Use supplied constraints and identify the actual contribution and audience. Ask about a missing limit or format only when it changes the requested work. Local copyediting need not become a full structural review.
2. For structural revision, identify what the reader cannot yet recover and what repeats. Sentence/job annotation can help diagnose a tangled draft; keep it optional and internal unless requested.
3. Order the necessary context and evidence so the contribution is understandable. Explain an unfamiliar approach before its result; combine familiar methods with findings when that is clearer. A completed study needs its actual result, not only its objective. Label incomplete work accurately.
4. Compress repeated setup and secondary detail before deleting method identity, the measured quantity, comparator or a qualification that changes the conclusion. Use only supplied findings and validation.
5. Read the result independently of the title, then compare it with the final paper. Check terminology, scope, evidence and the actual destination's requirements.

## Decision rules

- Test a generic opening by temporarily removing it. Cut or sharpen it if understanding is unchanged; retain it when it supplies context the intended reader needs. A long opening can be useful, and a reusable field fact is not automatically irrelevant.
- For a method, tool, dataset or resource, explain what it is, its distinctive capability and its name when supplied. Report demonstrations or benchmarks actually performed; do not manufacture comparative performance. If biological observations merely demonstrate a method, emphasize the capability. If they are a substantial second contribution, retain their findings and evidence rather than treating them as expendable detail.
- Preserve important method, system and comparison terms. Explain an essential technical term's function or use a familiar equivalent only when it means the same thing. An unfamiliar abbreviation is not a substitute for explanation.
- Give quantities with their comparator and conditions when they help identify the contribution. Preserve a limitation whose omission would misrepresent the answer. More numbers are not automatically more informative.
- Replace unsupported impact language with the demonstrated contribution. If the final result already explains what was learned, a separate “so what” sentence may only repeat it. A possible use must remain prospective.
- Follow actual structured headings and counting rules. Broad-to-specific-to-broad organization is a useful option for broad audiences, not a universal sequence. Archived examples and provisional drafting budgets do not establish current journal limits.

## Variants and edge cases

- For a very short abstract, combine functions and preserve what makes the contribution assessable; do not enforce a sentence quota or sacrifice the finding to a closing slogan.
- A specialist audience may need a precise technical opening; a broader audience may need more context and functional explanation. Neither needs a generic claim about the importance of all biology.
- A review or thesis abstract should convey its actual synthesis or combined contribution. It need not imitate an experimental paper's method/result sequence.
- Standalone abstracts usually avoid dependencies on figures or a separate bibliography. Check actual venue exceptions and define necessary abbreviations independently of their definition in the paper.

## Anti-patterns

- Filling a template with an invented gap, benchmark or benefit.
- Treating every methods paper as exclusively methodological.
- Removing necessary context because the opening is long or familiar.
- Listing activities instead of the completed work's contribution.
- Claiming that a shorter abstract or particular wording guarantees citations, acceptance or reader understanding.

## Diagnostic questions

Use only questions relevant to the requested edit: Can the reader identify the contribution and why it matters? Is enough approach/evidence present to understand it? Does compression preserve the comparison and its limits? Does the abstract make sense without the title? An optional outside reader can expose unfamiliar terms; do not claim that comprehension testing occurred unless it did.

## Output patterns / mini-templates

Return the requested rewrite. When useful, add a short note identifying a consequential missing fact or a change in claim strength. A structural diagnosis can use `reader need -> missing or repeated information -> revision`; it need not produce labels for every sentence. Any draft scaffold is provisional, not a source of facts.

## Examples

Each pair below is a fictional teaching case. Use the fixed facts stated for that pair; the sparse before sentences alone do not establish the added gap, design or outcome. If equivalent facts are absent from a user's material, preserve the known text and mark missing details.

Fixed facts: cell-type resolution of early developmental enhancer changes is unresolved; the study profiles enhancer-linked transcription across staged samples and finds recurrent regulatory shifts at early lineage transitions.

Before: `Many studies have investigated gene regulation in development. We used sequencing to examine enhancer activity.`

After: `How enhancer activity changes across early development remains difficult to resolve at cell-type resolution. We profiled enhancer-linked transcription across staged samples and found recurrent regulatory shifts at early lineage transitions.`

Fixed facts: CellMark combines local intensity cues with shape priors to segment touching cells; three benchmark datasets show improved instance recovery and preserved cell-size estimates in dense microscopy images.

Before: `Here we present a new computational method and show that it works better than existing tools.`

After: `CellMark combines local intensity cues with shape priors to segment touching cells. Across three benchmark datasets of dense microscopy images, CellMark improved instance recovery while preserving cell-size estimates.`

Changed condition: if the supplied work only demonstrates CellMark on images and provides no comparative benchmark, describe that demonstration and omit comparative claims such as “improved.” If a separately supported biological finding is also a central contribution, retain its result and comparison rather than applying a method-only word allocation. Neither change permits inventing the missing evaluation or biological result.

Fixed facts: the unresolved question concerns transcriptional changes before age-related loss of vaccine responsiveness; immune-cell transcriptomes were profiled before and after vaccination in younger and older adults; a pre-existing inflammatory program predicted weaker response. The design supports association.

Before: `This study investigated immune aging using transcriptomics and found many changes.`

After: `Which transcriptional changes precede age-related loss of vaccine responsiveness remains unclear. We profiled immune-cell transcriptomes before and after vaccination in younger and older adults and found that a pre-existing inflammatory program predicted weaker response. These results identify an early molecular state associated with reduced vaccine responsiveness in aging.`

## When not to apply this

Do not use this as the main playbook for full introductions, discussions, or figure legends; route those sections to their own reference files.

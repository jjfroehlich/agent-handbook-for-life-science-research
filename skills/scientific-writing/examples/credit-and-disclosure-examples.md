# Credit and disclosure: decisions before wording

These fictional cases supply their own facts. The output must not infer contributions, permission, approval or tool use from a vague before sentence. Apply the actual policy when working on a real manuscript.

## Substantial contribution with criteria still unresolved

**Supplied facts:** A made a substantial contribution to experimental design and substantive intellectual revisions. The applicable policy requires substantial research contribution, drafting/intellectual revision, final approval and accountability together. A has not yet been offered the final version or asked about accountability. Naming permission is available.

**Request:** “Make this acknowledgement sound professional: we thank A for all the help.”

**Useful response:**

> Before settling this as an acknowledgement, check A's eligibility under the supplied policy. The design and revision work may satisfy its first two criteria; final approval and accountability remain unconfirmed. Give A a real opportunity to review/approve the final version and accept the required responsibility. A working record is: “A contributed to experimental design and substantive manuscript revision.”

This does not pronounce A eligible, invent final approval or exclude A because the remaining steps have not been offered. Permission to name someone is a different question.

**Changed condition:** A supplied only a permitted equipment loan and explicitly made no other research or writing contribution. A possible acknowledgement is: “We thank A for lending the imaging equipment.” Equipment provision alone does not establish all four criteria; the same wording should not be used to conceal additional qualifying work if the facts change.

## Contribution roles do not certify outcomes

**Supplied facts:** B wrote analysis software and maintained the metadata needed to interpret the dataset. C prepared the visualizations. No independent reproduction or manuscript review is reported.

**Contribution draft:**

> B: Software; Data curation. C: Visualization.

Keep the two code-related activities distinct because both are supplied. Do not add Validation, Writing – review & editing or an all-author approval statement. A validation activity would describe a person's work, not prove that the results were successfully reproduced. Eligibility and order still need the applicable criteria and actual facts.

## AI assistance without invented all-author review

**Supplied facts:** Tool T suggested grammar and phrasing changes to two sentences. J checked those changes against the original scientific meaning. No analysis or figure generation was involved, and final coauthor review has not been established.

**Working disclosure wording, subject to the destination's requirements:**

> Tool T was used to suggest grammar and phrasing changes to two sentences. J checked those changes against the original scientific meaning.

**Separate unresolved release check:** confirm any further required disclosure details and actual author approvals. Human responsibility remains; that responsibility is not evidence that every author has already reviewed or edited the output. Do not write “all authors reviewed, edited and approved” from these facts.

**Changed condition:** T also generated analysis code. The writing-only disclosure is now incomplete. Record that actual use, the scientific/code checks actually performed and any unresolved checks; use the required analysis/methods disclosure location. Do not infer that a plausible result or a proposed checking plan establishes substantive verification.

## Unknown order and a departing contributor

**Supplied facts:** A team has agreed the research question but cannot predict the final contribution balance. One member may leave before drafting. No final order, post-departure access or approval contact is agreed.

**Draft planning note:**

> Author order remains provisional. We need to agree the contribution criteria and decision process, record actual work as it develops, and identify who will draft and obtain approvals after the planned departure. Data/material access and credit after departure also remain to be agreed.

This records open questions rather than claiming the message itself constitutes agreement. An early plan cannot waive later eligibility, contribution or approval requirements. If a power imbalance makes raising concerns directly risky, consider appropriate independent advice where available; do not promise confidentiality or make supervisor permission a prerequisite.

# Abstract Checklist

Use for fast abstract review before returning a rewrite.

- Venue constraints: use supplied word limits, headings and audience. Ask for a missing requirement only when it changes the requested work; do not invent a destination's rules.
- Reader needs: context, purpose, approach, findings and meaning are recoverable as needed, without compulsory sentence labels or an invented gap.
- Opening: it supplies context or a problem the intended reader needs; deleting it does not silently remove a premise.
- Approach: the method or design is described only at the level needed to understand the result.
- Result: the central finding, direction, or capability is explicit.
- Evidence: key evidence, validation, or comparison is present when available.
- Methods-paper variant: method identity and capability are clear; validation or benchmarks appear only when supplied. A substantive biological contribution retains its evidence in a hybrid paper.
- Searchability: important method names, systems, and comparison terms were not cut during compression.
- Interpretation: claims are bounded by the evidence; a possible use remains prospective, and the ending need not repeat the contribution.
- Brevity: repetition and irrelevant detail are reduced while necessary context, technical identity and conditions remain.
- Standalone clarity: the abstract makes sense without the title or main text.
- Recoverability: the reader can identify the actual contribution and the context, approach or implication needed to understand it; not every abstract needs each job stated separately.
- Accuracy: no rewrite changes uncertainty, scope, causal strength, or statistical direction.

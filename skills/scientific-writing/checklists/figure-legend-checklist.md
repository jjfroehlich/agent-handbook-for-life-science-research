# Figure Legend Checklist

Use for publication-readiness review of figure legends.

- Title: use an accurate finding or neutral subject/analysis phrase; do not require a result-first opening.
- Stage: a brief finding explanation may help when the title or multiple panels/results need it; neither initial explanation nor final shortening is universal. Follow the actual destination.
- Panel map: every panel letter is described in order.
- Main-text alignment: every panel cited in the text maps to the correct panel and claim.
- Visual definitions: colors, symbols, labels, abbreviations, and axes are defined.
- Conditions: controls, treatments, groups, time points, or sample types are identifiable.
- Evidence: the legend states what comparison the reader should inspect.
- Statistics: use actual tests, thresholds, error-bar definitions and sample/replicate information; do not infer independent biological replication from nested cells or images.
- Self-containment: the legend can be understood without searching the main text.
- Scope: retain necessary local context and accessible essential detail without repeating Results or the entire Methods section.
- Exact values: a related graph does not make a table redundant when it supplies needed exact values or different information.
- Consistency: terminology matches the main text and figure labels.
- Missing information: absent figure details are flagged rather than invented.
- Reviewer inspection: check comparison, condition, sample context and statistical notation at the intended size. An optional reader/sketch check is not completed comprehension testing.

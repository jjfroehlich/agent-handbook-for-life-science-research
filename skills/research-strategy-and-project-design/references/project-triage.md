# Project Triage

## Use This When

Use this playbook when the user has a fresh idea, an overgrown project, a disliked commitment, or a portfolio of possible directions that needs prioritization.

## The Job Of This Topic

Convert enthusiasm, obligation, and uncertainty into a decision process. The output should identify what to pursue, pause, pivot, or quit, and why.

## Default workflow

1. If fresh excitement prevents appraisal, record the idea and revisit it; do not impose a fixed waiting period.
2. Screen for impact, feasibility, timeline, technology risk, and fit with the user's role.
3. Name the uncertainties that change the next commitment; several may matter, and not all have a cheap test.
4. For a running project, review at a date or evidence/resource change that can affect the next commitment.
5. For evolving projects, compare useful branches and deliberately select which to pursue or set aside; keep a brief record when coordination or later reconsideration needs it.

## Decision rules

- Compare prospective value and cost with credible alternatives and existing commitments. Past investment, outside encouragement and an identity of persistence do not settle the next decision.
- Imagine the paper, dataset, tool, or result the project is becoming; if that output would not be worth the remaining work, stop or reshape.
- Separate project evolution from project sprawl: new branches are useful only when selected against goals and constraints.
- Consider whether the portfolio fits actual capacity and purposes. Feasible progress, learning and ambitious work can play different roles; no fixed mix, number of areas or publication quota is required. One focused project can be appropriate.

## Variants And Edge Cases

- A starter project can provide useful learning without defining a lifetime direction. Fit its scope and support to the person; use a review point when it helps reconsider an uncertain commitment.
- A project with a specific technical obstacle may warrant a bounded repair if support and access exist. A sound result against a prediction instead calls for revising that explanation. An imprecise result may leave both possibilities open.
- A resource-driven pause can be justified while the scientific question remains unanswered; do not keep ordering experiments merely to earn permission to stop.
- Collaborator opportunities can open branches, but they also create coordination and ownership costs.

## Planning an uncertain commitment

Use the smallest plan that exposes purpose, scope, assumptions, near-term actions, resources and dependencies. Detail uncertain later work progressively. Check each person's total load and distinguish elapsed time from hands-on work: access queues, culture growth, training and feedback can control the schedule. Review actual findings, deviations and causes, then change goals or estimates rather than repeatedly promise the same date. Monthly review is one option for extended work; the decision timescale or a lost premise may require another interval.

Include analysis, writing and external review when planning completion. When an uncertain project may miss the goal, compare routes with and without it and agree a useful switch point. A work breakdown, timeline, progress form or contingency table is an optional aid, not a discovery guarantee or universal paperwork requirement. Do not count long hours or promised positive findings as the milestone.

## Shared work and changed opportunities

Choose independent work, service, data/tool reuse or an integrated team according to the scientific task. Clarify particular interests, expertise, deliverables, real analysis/maintenance/compute labor, data access and coordination before relying on another person. A computational handoff may require iteration; a sufficiently hybrid researcher or suitable reuse route may need no extra team. Obtain actual authority for sensitive data and sharing; deidentification or a federated method alone establishes neither permission nor privacy.

For a collective decision, clarify disciplinary terms and assumptions. Connect the revised direction to actual contributions and constraints, welcome scientific disagreement and obtain relevant perspectives without treating cohesion as evidence. Diverse expertise can help only through workable participation and integration; status, late hours or an organizational chart do not establish commitment or useful access. SWOT or a network map can reveal a concrete dependency, but neither is a validated project score.

When work stalls, distinguish scientific obstacles, changed priorities or personal capacity, lost expertise and actual breaches before choosing repair or exit. Discuss effects on materials, data, credit and collaborators rather than assume a changed question erases commitments. A new publication may answer the original question while leaving a worthwhile comparison or replication; reassess what remains. Lost priority can have real costs and does not guarantee either worthlessness or a publishable remainder.

Include paid time, living responsibilities and usable support in feasibility. Do not turn dedication, stress or mental-health status into an eligibility judgment, or assume one supervisor is the only possible source of help. A commercial or institutional stop can be reasonable without disproving the scientific idea.

## Anti-Patterns

- Continuing because the user has already invested heavily.
- Continuing because quitting feels like a character flaw.
- Treating another person's advice as decisive without explaining the time and resource costs they may not know.
- Pursuing every branch opened by new data or collaborators.

## Diagnostic Questions

- What decision must be made now: pursue, narrow, pivot, pause, or quit?
- What would the final output look like if the project succeeds?
- Is there a credible affordable check that could change the next commitment, or does the useful information require a larger investment?
- What alternative project would use the same time and resources?
- Which branches are being kept because they are promising, and which because no one chose to close them?

## Output Patterns / Mini-Templates

When a running project needs an explicit shared record, select useful fields such as:

```text
Current commitment:
Remaining scientific contribution:
Remaining cost:
Main uncertainty:
Stop/go review date:
Continue if:
Stop or pivot if:
Next informative action or reason to pause/stop:
```

## Examples

- If project value depends on a technology, test that dependency early when a credible affordable check exists; otherwise compare the actual investment with alternatives.
- If the user dislikes a starter project and cannot name a worthwhile output, a short reassessment may help; a justified stop need not wait for another experiment or review cycle.
- If many branches emerged from early data, compare their expected learning and costs with the supplied priorities before adding commitments; ask only for missing facts that could change the choice.

## When Not To Apply This

Do not use project triage to dismiss obligations that have ethical, supervisory, contractual, or collaboration commitments without separately addressing those constraints.

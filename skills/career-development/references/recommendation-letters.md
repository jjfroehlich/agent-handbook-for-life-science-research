# Recommendation Letters

## Use this when...

Use this when the user has been asked to draft, scaffold, or supply evidence for a recommendation letter, reference letter, award nomination, fellowship letter, visa-support letter, or recommender packet.

## The job of this topic

Help the user provide useful evidence without pretending to be the recommender. The user's job is to make contribution, context, and criteria easy to use; the recommender owns final voice, comparative judgment, and endorsement strength.

## Default workflow

1. Identify the opportunity, level, deadline, review criteria, and recommender relationship.
2. Ask what evidence the recommender personally observed and what the user can document.
3. Build an evidence scaffold: relationship context, role, specific contributions, outputs, impact, and stage-appropriate qualities.
4. Separate candidate-supplied facts from recommender-owned judgment.
5. Remove unsupported superlatives, negatives, private jokes, and claims the recommender cannot verify.
6. Offer a concise packet or scaffold rather than a finished letter in someone else's voice.

## Decision rules

- If the user was asked to draft their own letter, provide structure and evidence, not a final endorsement.
- If the draft lists techniques, revise toward distinctive contributions, role, impact, and judgment.
- If the opportunity is high-stakes, include criteria and evidence that help the recommender calibrate level.
- If multiple recommenders need input, provide a shared evidence packet and tailor relationship context for each person.
- If the user wants to include a weakness or caveat, leave that decision to the recommender.

## Variants and edge cases

- Trainee applications should emphasize growth, promise, evidence of skill, and support observed by the recommender.
- Postdoc or fellowship letters need independence, project ownership, judgment, and fit with the opportunity.
- Faculty, award, or leadership letters should foreground independent contribution, field-level impact, mentoring, service, and future trajectory.
- Visa or credentialing contexts may need a short recommender-credential block, but the user should only supply accurate facts.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing a fake final letter in the recommender's voice.
- Choosing endorsement strength, ranking, or comparative claims for the recommender.
- Using unsupported superlatives without evidence.
- Listing skills without explaining the contribution or impact.
- Including negatives, apologies, or self-undermining caveats.
- Sending identical narratives to multiple recommenders without context.

## Diagnostic questions

- What decision will this letter help a committee make?
- What did this recommender personally observe?
- Which contribution is most distinctive for the user's stage?
- What evidence shows judgment, independence, creativity, rigor, communication, or leadership?
- What final judgment belongs only to the recommender?

## Output patterns / mini-templates

Recommendation scaffold:

```text
Opportunity and criteria:
Recommender relationship and observed context:
Opening summary placeholder:
Contribution 1: evidence, role, impact:
Contribution 2: evidence, role, impact:
Qualities supported by evidence:
Stage calibration:
Optional context the recommender may adapt:
Recommender-owned judgment to leave blank:
```

Evidence packet row:

| Section | Candidate-supplied evidence | Recommender-owned judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship | Worked together in course/lab/project during X period | How close and credible the recommender's view is |
| Contribution | Specific result, role, output, or decision | How impressive that contribution is for the stage |
| Attribute | Example showing judgment, independence, rigor, or leadership | Final strength, ranking, and endorsement language |

## Examples

- Instead of "I am outstanding," provide a project example, your exact role, the obstacle, the outcome, and why it matters for the opportunity.
- Instead of writing the recommender's closing endorsement, leave a placeholder such as "final comparative endorsement supplied by recommender."

## When not to apply this

Do not use this playbook to forge, impersonate, or pressure a recommender. If the user needs a letter from their own voice, use cover-letter or application-material guidance instead.

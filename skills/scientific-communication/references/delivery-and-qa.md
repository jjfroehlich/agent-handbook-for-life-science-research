# Delivery and questions

Use for voiced rehearsal, time management, setup, accessible participation, speaking support or scientific Q&A. A local delivery problem can be repaired directly; structural revision is needed only when the problem reveals a missing explanation or overloaded route.

## Rehearse to learn what needs revision

Rehearse aloud early enough to change the talk. Include the spoken opening, transitions, visual work and ending; silent reading and slide count cannot establish duration. Use notes, a precise script, personally useful checkpoints or an optional recording as needed. A suitable candid listener may notice premises that project colleagues silently supply. Ask what comparison supported the claim and where the connection disappeared.

Revise between runs. If time runs long, remove a detour or secondary topic, then rehearse the surviving transitions. Preserve decisive evidence, limitations and the close, and leave the discussion time the session actually allows. A delay before the start can permit a different route; a late time loss may require a concise supported conclusion with an accurate account of evidence not shown. Do not speed through necessary science or pretend an omitted experiment has been completed.

Useful improvisation and scripted precision can coexist. Feedback works within available time, permission and speaker ownership; a late unwanted overhaul may be declined. If retaining questions would help, arrange a consenting note-taker or another feasible record rather than assume the speaker can remember everything. Repetition counts, preparation ratios, attention windows and training doses are heuristics, not validated prescriptions or guaranteed mastery.

Once a passage is secure, remove presenter-only reminders if they compete with the audience's view, while keeping private notes, precise wording and genuine audience labels or uncertainty. A read-then-discuss document, small-group board or detailed self-paced account may serve a different encounter better than a sparse live deck.

## Support the speaker and the explanation

Give listeners time to inspect unfamiliar axes, locate conditions and follow a change in state. Use a spoken cue, label, pointer or annotation for the actual feature. A pointer should not be the only way to recover a necessary comparison. Pauses and purposeful changes in voice, pace or energy can help the particular speaker; louder, more theatrical or continuously audience-facing delivery is not automatically better.

Choose support for the actual barrier. A practiced opening, notes, a pause, optional recording or directing attention toward the audience's question can be useful. Nerves need not disappear. Gaze, posture, smiling, mobility, an accent and visible confidence are not competence or sincerity tests. For an ordinary gaffe, pause, acknowledge or reset when useful and return to the explanation. A material scientific error, offense or misattribution needs an honest correction. Do not diagnose the speaker or promise a clinical or guaranteed confidence effect.

## Check the actual setup and fallback

When feasible, check sightlines, sound, projection/shared screen, presenter view, cables/power, controls and the relevant files. Learn how this session works and request preparation time if needed. Retain an editable master; choose vector/raster for the content and destination, remembering embedded raster limits. Test exported fonts, playback and important transitions on the equipment or platform that will be used.

Prepare independent backups for plausible failures. Notes, an offline/static copy, host equipment or a personal setup cover different failures; a no-slide explanation may not preserve a visual comparison. For a failure-prone software demonstration, compare genuine live execution, a prerecorded screencast with live/recorded commentary and prepared commands. Disclose recorded or simulated material and actual execution status. Never fake live scientific results. A checklist or a previous successful run is not evidence that the current setup works.

A lectern can support access, ceremony or notes; a demonstration may require working at the computer. Remove a barrier only when feasible and useful. A blank display, object or live drawing can focus an explanation when the required evidence remains available and the object is actually visible or magnified for the audience.

## Make participation usable

For a group or remote/hybrid discussion, establish the purpose, useful context, agenda, speaking/question route and feasible time. Give remote and in-room participants a way to hear, inspect the material and contribute. Quietness, a camera being off, device use and visible attention do not establish agreement, inattention or understanding.

When checking understanding would improve the explanation, invite predictions, explanations or reasoning about data. Allow thought time and accessible response alternatives rather than rely only on the quickest volunteers. Use low-stakes responses to locate a misconception and revise the explanation. Ask for reasons and correct material errors; a majority vote does not establish scientific truth. This is communication feedback, not a claim of a measured learning effect or a complete course-assessment method.

Clarify consequential requests and proposed next actions without equating transmission, receipt or discussion with consent. Chair within actual authority, check before extending time and allow follow-up rather than force disclosure or continuous participation. Panels may benefit from coordinated framing and different voices when prepared for the task; do not manufacture dialogue or agreement.

## Answer the scientific question

Let the question finish. Clarify or restate only as needed, preserving its meaning; pause and answer the substance. Separate what the evidence supports, your speculation, an unknown and an actual disclosure limit. A related result can help after the unanswered part is made explicit; it cannot substitute for an answer to a different question.

Accept valid correction and explain its effect on the claim. Correct a false premise where needed, but leave a genuine validity objection open until addressed. Do not invent a control, preliminary result, replication, confidentiality reason or availability. An honest unknown can be followed by a clearly proposed test or a feasible follow-up, not a promise of work already done.

Use genuine navigable reserve evidence, adapting technical depth to the question and room. For prolonged disruption, use proportionate chair/moderation help, a later discussion or an appropriate pause. Respect does not require harassment endurance, agreement with a false claim or evading legitimate criticism. Recurring questions can reveal a needed explanation; their number is not a comprehension score.

## Share only a useful, permitted account

Choose among an annotated deck, voice-plus-slide recording, conference video, short context introduction, companion document or no public copy. A bare deck may lose spoken context; some decks already stand alone. Inspect the actual recording and access path before saying it works or is published. Exclude private presenter notes, unreleased material and other people's information unless authorized.

Credit actual major, minor and coordination contributions where relevant, checking omissions across career levels. Concise credit placement cannot erase contributors or settle authorship. A citation, public availability, search filter or noncommercial label does not establish permission to reuse an asset. Read [Public explanations and media](public-and-media.md) for release constraints, reporter encounters and controversy; a proposed correction or contact is not a completed action.

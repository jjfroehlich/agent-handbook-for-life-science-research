# Talk structure

Use for a seminar, conference talk, technical update, public explanation or decision briefing. Start with the actual question the audience needs answered. A clear account can contain several independent questions, unresolved results or a proposed procedure.

## Choose the explanation path

| Audience task | Useful starting route | What changes the choice |
|---|---|---|
| Understand unfamiliar findings | Orient to the biological question and comparison, then connect evidence to answers | State a central finding early when listeners can interpret it; do not withhold an essential premise for suspense |
| Receive a familiar progress update | Give the supported answer or current status, then inspect its evidence and remaining questions | Separate independent threads instead of manufacturing one discovery |
| Choose between proposals | Explain the actual situation, complication and decision; give the proposed answer and reasons | If evidence cannot support an answer, compare unresolved alternatives and the information needed |
| Critique a method or troubleshoot | Introduce the intended operation, settings, observed problem and plausible alternatives | A method central to the task belongs in the main explanation, even if a broad findings talk could compress it |
| Understand a historical or reflective account | Preserve the real sequence and distinguish recollection from recorded evidence | An explanatory reorder is acceptable only if it does not change chronology or imply a false discovery history |

An audience's job title does not establish what it knows. Ask the host or use supplied context to identify shared concepts, unfamiliar techniques and the reason people will attend. Experts may need little introductory motivation and substantial discriminating detail. A mixed audience needs an accessible route to the question and inference while specialists receive the precision needed to evaluate them.

Keep audience knowledge separate from facts about the work. Familiarity with an assay can guide terminology, but does not establish that the reported experiment used it. Name a performed method, comparison or result only when the supplied experimental record supports it. When explaining an unfamiliar term, preserve its biological scope: do not turn a general category into a particular subtype or add a mechanism merely to simplify the wording. Distinguish general background from facts established for the reported system.

For a broader communication program, choose the intended understanding or decision, feasible effort, support and risks before choosing a channel. Low-key involvement can be appropriate. Read [Public explanations and media](public-and-media.md) for program planning, genre, release and interview decisions; a request for one slide does not require a communication campaign.

## Plan before polishing

When reorganizing a whole talk, draft the major questions first. Place each supporting comparison underneath its question, check the foundation across sections, then deepen the parts that need it. A sentence/evidence outline, rough visual sketches, a spoken script, an existing deck or a direct local repair can all work. Paper planning is an option, not a prerequisite.

Make the link into each result explicit: what was unknown, what comparison addresses it and what observation would distinguish the alternatives? Introduce an unfamiliar system, assay or graphical convention before asking listeners to interpret it. Explain axes, units and what each observation represents when needed; label an expected pattern as hypothetical. Familiar displays may need only a brief cue.

A conclusion-led briefing can group reasons beneath a governing answer. Read the title sequence and inspect each body's evidence: do they support the promised argument? Branch counts and strict nonoverlap are craft preferences, not proof that reasons are exhaustive or statistically independent. If proposing change, make the next decision, resources, costs, sacrifices, risks and proportionate possible benefits assessable. A justified objection may require revision, more evidence or withdrawal of the proposal.

## Shorten without changing the inference

Choose a main path the audience can actually follow in the allotted session. Cut redundant wording, an ancillary topic, unnecessary history or a second illustration before removing a decisive comparison, control, prerequisite or uncertainty. A result that changes the conclusion stays in the explanation; if it does not fit the story, change the story or scope. A coherent list or multi-result update is valid.

Use navigable backup, notes or a handout for genuine optional depth. Say accurately what was not shown. An unshown result is not necessarily a completed result, and an extension still needed to establish the main claim cannot be hidden in reserve. Rehearse the shorter route aloud: fewer slides alone do not establish that it fits. See [Delivery and questions](delivery-and-qa.md).

## Open, connect and close

Give enough context to understand the question, the task and the route through it. An unfamiliar seminar may need a setup; a requested specialist answer can begin directly. An agenda can help people navigate a program, but a list of section labels cannot substitute for explaining the relationships.

For a proposed conference session, distinguish the public program's truthful promise from a brief private organizer note about implementation. A broad theme can allow later development without promising results, learning or a visit that have not been established.

Use transitions that ask or answer the next scientific question. A recurring model, concise handle or local recap can orient a longer explanation without becoming a branding exercise or a fixed recap schedule. If you promise to explain something in the opening, make the promise feasible and check that the close answers it.

Protect an ending that distinguishes the supported answer, implications and remaining unknowns. A concluding display can support questions; a blank endpoint, question, invitation or compact summary may suit another session. Keep consequential limitations and credit visible. Humor, thanks, sentence counts and an action ask are choices, not required closing ingredients.

## Return useful work

For an outline, give the actual ordered questions, evidence needed beside each, spoken connections, conclusion or open decisions, optional depth and a rehearsal check. For a local repair, show the repaired transition or order and why it changes comprehension. State missing evidence that prevents a stronger recommendation; do not fill an outline with invented findings or a guessed audience biography.

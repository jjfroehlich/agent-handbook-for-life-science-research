# Storytelling and explanation

Use when the explanation lacks a connection, an unfamiliar concept needs a bridge, or the user asks for narrative framing. Start with the scientific relationship the audience must understand. Narrative can help explain it; a direct account, accurate list, chronology or specialist branch may serve the task better.

## Connect the actual questions and evidence

An optional narrative route is context, question/problem, attempt, evidence, interpretation and implication. A partial answer can raise the next question. An early answer followed by support is another legitimate route. Do not create suspense by withholding the essential point from listeners who need it to interpret the evidence.

As a drafting exercise, compress a relationship into a word or phrase, a sentence and a paragraph, or test whether an “and, but, therefore” connection exposes the argument. The literal connectors, one protagonist and a closed ending are optional. Restore enough system, comparison, work status and uncertainty for the final account; a conversational middle draft can be too empty to use. Several themes or no single theme may be accurate.

A rhetorical “therefore” cannot establish causality. Keep a probable mechanism probable and separate observation, proposed explanation and test. An inconclusive or negative result needs no fabricated positive resolution, prospective hypothesis or antagonist. Explain what the estimate and precision allow; nonsignificance alone does not establish absence. If contrary evidence disrupts the story, retain the evidence and revise the account.

Use a person or goal only when it clarifies real work. Preserve collaborators' roles and important trial-and-error history when process is the subject. A gradual career transition need not become a dramatic turning point, and a speaker need not disclose trauma or private experience. If collecting accounts would help, use a proportionate retrievable record, check recollections against contemporaneous material and relevant witnesses, and label reconstruction or fiction. Repeated recollection does not validate a fact or supply consent to share it.

## Build from familiar to unfamiliar

Identify the listener's actual starting knowledge and the concept needed next. Explain a technical label by its relevant role, then use a stable name. Everyday words such as “significant,” “expression” or “memory” can also carry specialist meanings. Retain terminology the audience understands; a blanket jargon ban can remove precision.

Across disciplines, check seemingly shared terms, methods and assumptions as well. Familiar words can conceal different meanings even among experts; clarify the difference rather than erase a necessary scientific distinction.

Choose among a concrete example, comparison, analogy, description, diagram or direct explanation. For an analogy, name what maps to what and where the correspondence stops. Return to the actual biological system for evidence. If an intentional creature or machine implies a belief, desire, purpose or mechanism that has not been established, identify the metaphorical departure or replace it. Do not assume listeners can recover the rigorous mechanism from the metaphor unaided.

Check quantities, units, geometry, uncertainty, timing and image origin during simplification. Transparent approximation can help when the task permits it; replacing53% with “half,” changing a frequency or using a photograph from another occasion can change the claim. Do not replace a longer word with a stronger causal one. A vivid invented detail cannot rescue a real scientific account.

## Make the wording preserve the relationship

Check who did what, when, under which condition and with what evidence. “If” leaves a condition open; “when” can imply it will occur. A participial phrase may make sequential operations sound simultaneous. Keep connectors and referents clear instead of varying technical names for style.

Questions can set up a real comparison, declaratives can state its answer and commands can guide inspection. Parallel form helps readers compare genuinely comparable quantities; it must not force asymmetric evidence, a continuum or several causes into a false binary. Ellipsis is useful only if the missing units or referent remain recoverable. Letter-shape, sentence-count or grade-level recipes cannot certify understanding.

## Choose the medium and test the explanation

A discovery news item, unfinished-work feature, explanatory page, Q&A, opinion, talk or audio account need different arrangements. Read [Public explanations and media](public-and-media.md) for genre, channel and factual-check choices. An educational account can end at its supported takeaway without inventing an application or ask.

Try the explanation with a suitable reader/listener when feasible. Ask what the main comparison establishes and what remains unknown. Enjoyment, applause, a remembered phrase or many questions do not by themselves show understanding, learning, agreement or trust. Treat feedback as a reason to revise a specific omission or misleading relationship, not as proof that a narrative formula works.

Return the requested revised explanation with its actual claim boundary. If a premise is missing, identify it or offer explicitly conditional wording instead of inventing a discovery or result. Keep teaching commentary outside the sample speech.

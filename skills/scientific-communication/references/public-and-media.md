# Public explanations and media

Use for choosing a public explanation's genre/channel, planning a communication program, preparing release/media access, an interview or a controversy response. Use the supplied facts and actual constraints; preparation does not establish authorization to contact anyone or publish. This reference provides communication choices, not legal, clinical or institutional determinations.

## Decide what the communication should accomplish

Identify the audience, desired understanding or decision, feasible effort, support and risks before selecting channels. Education, a professional account and advocacy have different endpoints. Limited or low-key involvement can be appropriate; every researcher need not become a media performer.

For a continuing program, connect genuine milestones to suitable material and available collaborators. Reuse verified work after adapting its context, depth and genre, without inventing results or institutional commitments. Allocate real updating, responding and correction work. Review the role as capacity and circumstances change; repetition cannot guarantee adoption.

Ask what recipients understood as well as checking accuracy. Attention, enjoyment, views, publicity and agreement are distinct from learning, trust, scientific validity or benefit. A count of clicks or positive responses cannot establish the intended outcome.

## Choose the genre and medium

| Material and purpose | Possible route | Necessary check |
|---|---|---|
| A timely supported finding | News account stating what was found, why it matters and why now promptly | Keep scope, uncertainty and actual novelty intact |
| Work still unfolding or a background question | Feature, reported scene, layered explanation or Q&A | Do not imply a completed result; preserve the factual basis of a scene |
| A supported position and genuine proposed action | Clearly identified opinion and remedy | Distinguish evidence, values, costs and the actual next decision |
| A needed process, motion or human context | Video, animation or narrated slides | The visual must add explanation; inspect actual playback and permissions |
| An account useful without visuals | Audio or a direct text explanation | It must work without an unseen diagram or essential missing context |
| Sustained exchange or changing background | Discussion, layered page or ongoing resource | Provide navigation/access and capacity for updates, responses and visible corrections |

A short update or informal post can direct interested readers to a fuller account; it does not replace the evidentiary explanation. Plan a rough message, storyboard or navigation before expensive production. Test representative understanding where feasible. Optional depth may contain extended detail, but it must not conceal claim-changing qualifications. Education need not acquire a fictional application, dramatic opening or action ask. Deletion, an informal channel or anonymity settings do not guarantee that material disappears or remains anonymous.

Choose medium, delivery platform and distribution separately: needed motion does not establish that the audience can access the chosen host or attend at one time. Consider resources, time zones and readable/captioned alternatives, and test the delivered prototype. A polished editable file alone does not establish usable access at the destination.

A feature can begin with an accurately reported scene, broaden into the question and evidence, then return to a focused account; hard news generally needs its main finding promptly. Do not invent sensory detail, thoughts or dialogue to supply that scene. A sustained book needs scope and production capacity that a shorter explanation may not require. A partnership with an educator or media specialist can be more useful than doing every genre yourself; agree responsibilities and provide accurate subject resources without assuming unpaid or already-agreed work.

## Establish what can be released or accessed

Verify applicable coauthor, institutional, contractual, privacy and timing constraints with the responsible people when needed. Publishing a paper and publicizing it can involve different permissions. “Internal” does not guarantee containment. Do not infer permission from public availability, a draft approval, a citation or a noncommercial purpose; check actual asset rights and material that involves others.

Choose recipients for their topic/audience need and coordinate multi-institution credit and feasible spokesperson availability. Provide permitted useful background without unnecessary obstacles. Advance interviews for preparation can preserve the same agreed embargo when actual rules permit contact; do not secretly let a favored outlet publish early while asking others to wait. An individually pitched feature can involve a different access agreement. If a reporter is already investigating and may publish independently, clarify the reporting timetable and seek an authorized timing resolution with the responsible communicators rather than assume a delay is agreed. These are different promises about preparation, access and publication. Confirm current terms instead of borrowing a historical workaround or promising incompatible times.

Use selected relevant contact rather than indiscriminate repeated distribution. A feature pitch, an overlooked important alert or permitted stakeholder follow-through after coverage can serve a different purpose. Prominence does not prove receipt. A proposed contact, correction or redistribution has neither occurred nor gained authorization merely because it appears in a plan.

## Prepare and answer an interview

Find out the outlet, question, format, deadline and agreed use/attribution terms. For an unexpected call, obtain the scope and deadline and arrange a feasible response rather than improvise unknown facts. Do not assume that “off the record” applies, that factual checking gives editorial control or that a restart guarantees deletion of the earlier recording.

Prepare a concise what/why account plus the real concepts, caveats, controversies and likely challenges. Adapt print, broadcast, written answers or longer observation to what can actually be conveyed. Answer legitimate difficult questions within the evidence. Distinguish your own ignorance, an unknown in the field, fact, opinion and policy values. Omit unsupported primacy; adding “possibly” cannot repair an unverified claim to be first.

When communicating risk, keep the actual baseline, absolute quantities, denominator/rate period and consequential qualifications. Do not invent a baseline or replace an individual result with an implied clinical or population benefit. If those facts are absent, identify what cannot yet be said.

Ask the interviewer to explain the meaning back when useful. Make factual checks and request proportionate correction of material errors or credit omissions through a feasible route. State what was requested and what was actually corrected. A promise, proposed letter or review opportunity does not guarantee publication, consent or repaired relations.

## Respond to controversy with the actual evidence

Prepare verified background, consequential limitations, relevant influences and actual comment constraints. Distinguish established facts, allegations, speculation, a planned investigation and a completed remedy. Explain what is known and being checked without unsupported reassurance, threats, invented action or a guarantee of favorable coverage.

Consider how stakeholders understand the situation and examine your own assumptions. Emotion can matter to communication without proving a scientific claim or another person's intent. Correct consequential misunderstandings and credit errors accurately through an appropriate permitted route. Prompt candor does not override privacy or actual legal, ethical and institutional restrictions; specialist advice may clarify options but cannot guarantee protection, confidentiality or an outcome.

Return the requested explanation, interview preparation, bounded response or release plan. Mark missing permissions and proposed actions plainly. Do not send, publish, promise a finding or claim to have repaired a relationship when only a draft has been prepared.

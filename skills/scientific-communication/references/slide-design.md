# Slide design

Use for slide layout, titles, figure adaptation, explanatory diagrams, staged construction or deck mechanics. Inspect the supplied slide or rendered deck before judging appearance. From an outline alone, give a proposed arrangement and identify what still needs visual inspection.

## Make the comparison visible

Name what viewers need to inspect. Give related evidence enough shared space to compare it; use position, size, contrast, proximity and alignment to distinguish its roles. A slide can hold two plots, an image and its quantification, or several alternatives when their relationship is the point. Split independent conclusions when one crowds the other, rather than imposing a panel or message quota.

Choose the visual form for the relationship. A process, timeline, hierarchy, grouping, location/cutaway and contextual number need different arrangements. A richer overview can be necessary when relationships would otherwise disappear. Sketch alternatives and ask someone to describe the relationship they see. Decorative photos, stock arrows, face/gaze imagery and novelty do not establish understanding.

Keep names, colors, positions and scales consistent where viewers track an entity or compare conditions. Explain a consequential scale or mapping change. A coherent family of photographs/components can reduce needless variation, but scientific geometry and actual image information must survive. Brief a designer on the intended meaning and consolidate feedback while retaining subject-expert responsibility. Repeated group production can benefit from flexible content-based components with a reason for each; a small local task does not need a template system.

## Adapt evidence faithfully

Enlarge the needed panels and labels, simplify a manuscript legend into usable nearby cues, and explain what the marks represent. Retain units, group identities, denominators, sample/statistical units, dependence, controls and uncertainty needed for the displayed inference. If these do not fit legibly, enlarge the evidence or spread the explanation across views. Removing ticks, a control or raw image context to make a cleaner slide can destroy the comparison.

Show observations/distribution when spread matters; an appropriate interval for estimated-mean precision can serve a different question. Define the interval rather than ban standard error, and never turn nonsignificance into equivalence. Visual impressions do not replace the relevant analysis. A table suits exact lookup; one contextual quantity may need no graph, whereas comparing relationships needs an adequate display. Decorative perspective can obscure two-dimensional values; genuine three-dimensional science may require it.

Separate measured data, summaries, schematic illustrations, hypotheses and possible combinations. Preserve image information; disclose consequential composites or nonlinear processing under the applicable rules. Do not selectively obscure contrary features. A selected example does not establish representativeness. Check source dates, denominators and causal support before repeating polished sample numbers or business explanations as scientific evidence.

## Choose the title and text by their job

A specific supported finding title can orient scanning readers and a mixed audience. A question fits an unresolved comparison; a topic or concise fragment can identify a technical view; an intentional title-free display can support a spoken explanation when identification and access remain adequate. Inspect title-to-body agreement and the title sequence. An association or probable mechanism must not become causal or certain to produce a stronger headline.

Move extended spoken explanation into notes when listeners do not need it simultaneously. Retain necessary definitions, labels, credit and access support on screen. A quotation, definition, short passage or translated text may need visible words and time to read them; sparse prose and photographs are not universal requirements. Use clear hierarchy and intentional breaks for text that belongs. Give an essential translation legible space rather than treating it as disposable clutter.

## Decide what stays together and what appears in stages

Use a build when introducing the parts of an unfamiliar process in sequence. Keep earlier objects in place, preserve their identity and show the state/time change honestly. A staged schematic must not imply measured timing, a causal step or a completed experiment that the material does not establish.

For a simultaneous comparison, keep both conditions, models or states visible. Otherwise viewers must remember one while judging the other. A useful compromise is a progressive introduction followed by the complete comparison. Duplicate slides can provide a simple build, but revise every copy so old labels or values do not reappear.

Use motion only for a needed process, trajectory, timing or change of state. Plan what will be discussed while it plays, where it pauses and when it stops. A static sequence can be a better fit for the task or a fallback. Inspect actual playback and exported behavior before claiming either works; static screenshots cannot certify motion or interaction.

## Inspect at the destination

Check the smallest essential label, contrast, crowding and figure context at the expected viewing size and distance, including shared-screen conditions where relevant. No universal point size, word count, light/dark background or media count guarantees legibility. Pair color with labels, symbols or other distinguishable roles; a color-only distinction may disappear in projection or for a viewer.

Retain an editable master and choose vector/raster export for the content and destination. Embedded raster data remain resolution-limited inside a vector container. Test the actual file, fonts, controls and playback, and prepare a fallback appropriate to the possible failure; use [Delivery and questions](delivery-and-qa.md) for setup. Credit the work on the relevant slide and check actual reuse permission; an online image, citation or search filter is not a license. A standalone shared deck may need annotations or a companion account that the live version leaves to speech.

For a slide audit, identify the visible problem, the intended comparison, the specific rearrangement or wording change, what must remain, and the check needed after implementation. Do not report a redesign as implemented or readable until its actual artifact has been inspected.

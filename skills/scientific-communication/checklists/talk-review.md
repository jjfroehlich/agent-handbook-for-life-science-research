# Talk Review Checklist

Use for a final or fast review of a scientific talk, seminar, slide deck, pitch deck, or rehearsal plan.

Apply only checks relevant to the requested scope and available artifact. A static deck can support content/layout judgments, but cannot establish that rehearsal, playback, room setup or audience understanding has been tested. Mark those checks unverified when evidence is absent.

## Message And Audience

- The recommendation uses the supplied audience, purpose, format and venue; missing information is requested only when it could change the work.
- The talk has a clear purpose: a main message or distinct decision questions, rather than only a topic.
- The opening supplies the context needed to follow; a familiar requested technical answer need not add a ceremonial hook.
- Terms and background match the audience's starting knowledge.
- The close states what changed, why it matters, or what comes next.

## Structure

- The sequence fits the task: connected findings, decision questions or a small set of audience-relevant moves without an arbitrary result quota.
- Each section answers a clear audience question.
- Transitions explain why the next section follows.
- Methods and settings receive the depth the actual task needs; a troubleshooting or method-feedback session can center them.
- Backup material is separated from the main path.
- Decisive controls, comparisons and qualifications remain with the claim they support.

## Slides

- The displayed relationships are clear; evidence that needs simultaneous comparison remains together.
- Finding, question, topic or intentionally absent titles fit the task and do not strengthen the evidence.
- Paper figures are adapted for live viewing.
- Labels and key data are readable at the expected screen size.
- Color, contrast, and annotations guide attention without adding noise.
- Units, sample/statistical units, interval meaning, decisive controls and uncertainty survive simplification. Schematics and predictions are distinct from measurements.
- Staged views retain entity identity and earlier context; a full view is available when the comparison needs it. Playback remains unverified unless actually checked.

## Delivery And Q&A

- When within the requested scope, aloud rehearsal establishes timing rather than slide counts or silent reading.
- Opening, transitions, difficult slides, and close have been practiced.
- The plan includes timing milestones and cuts if running long.
- Likely questions have short, evidence-based answers.
- Unknowns and limitations are handled without overclaiming.
- Notes, scripts and delivery supports fit the speaker and setting; confidence, gaze or audience silence is not an evidence or comprehension test.
- Backups, recording/sharing permissions and actual access are checked before claiming they work or are authorized.
- A material validity objection is addressed rather than renamed as a tangent; a missing control stays missing.

## Highest-Leverage Fixes

Choose the repair that addresses the most consequential current failure; keep a working title or sequence when the problem lies elsewhere.

- Repair an unclear or overstated claim/question before styling it.
- Enlarge or rearrange unreadable decisive evidence and its control when listeners cannot inspect the comparison.
- Cut a secondary detour when density or time pressure prevents following the necessary explanation; preserve evidence and qualifications.
- Rehearse the opening, transitions, close or questions when the actual problem concerns timing, speech or recovery.

## Bad Talk Failure Check

- Did slide building start before audience, message, and explanation path were clear?
- Is the goal to explain the intended questions rather than impress?
- Can each slide justify its cognitive cost?
- Are jargon, side stories, and decorative animations serving the concept?
- Does rehearsal finish within time without rushing?
- Does Q&A preparation include listening, paraphrasing, direct answers, uncertainty, and concise closes?

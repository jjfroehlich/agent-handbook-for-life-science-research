# Hiring And Onboarding Checklist

## Before Hiring

- Define the role purpose and expected independence.
- List required skills and trainable skills.
- Identify supervision load and mentoring capacity.
- Write structured interview questions.
- Define evaluation criteria before reviewing candidates.
- Check funding runway, equipment, administrative support, PI time, and project readiness.
- Separate total appointment costs from teaching time and continuity needs; compare experienced and novice roles for this bottleneck rather than treating one stage as cheap labor.
- Check the actual appointment, reference and offer process with the responsible office; old salary, probation or visa examples are not current terms.
- Plan access, training and supervision around the role and appointment length; week/month/quarter intervals are possible starting points.

## Candidate Evaluation

- Use evidence tied to the rubric.
- Do not substitute prestige, visibility, or institution names for contribution.
- Check collaboration needs and complementary skills.
- Record concerns and support needs explicitly.
- Let candidates inspect actual resources and ask current members about the work; distinguish honest limitations from promised support. Obtain needed permission before contacting a current supervisor.

## Onboarding

- Provide the handbook and role-specific expectations.
- Assign first contacts and recurring meetings.
- Clarify communication, authorship, data/code, AI, safety, and help routes.
- Set a first project with realistic scope.
- Schedule early reviews around actual training and work milestones.
- Ask what remains unclear after onboarding.
- Check demonstrated readiness for the specific task; completed orientation or a supervised run is insufficient for independent responsibility.

## Collaboration And Undergraduate Fit

- Advertise deal-breakers, time requirements, prerequisites, and supervision cadence.
- Include a realistic project or presentation/Q&A step when appropriate.
- Ask for examples of collaboration, preparation, honest mistake handling, and persistence.
- Check observable job-relevant collaboration behavior without using personal similarity, prestige or unbounded availability as proxies.
- Identify everyday contacts; use a buddy when useful and supported.
- Discuss initial expectations and review them after the relevant training period.

# Conflict And Feedback Playbook

## Use this when...

Use this playbook for feedback scripts, missed expectations, interpersonal conflict, structural management failures, criticism norms, failed experiments, distress-sensitive conversations, and performance concerns.

## The job of this topic

Keep feedback rigorous and humane by diagnosing structure before blame. A conflict may be interpersonal, but it may also be caused by unclear expectations, role design, resource constraints, incentives, authorship rules, or power asymmetry.

## Default workflow

1. State the observed behavior or conflict without diagnosis.
2. Check how the expectation was communicated and understood, and whether it was role-specific and realistic. A written record is one input, not proof of understanding or consent.
3. Examine role, resources, training, workload, project fit, incentives, meeting norms, authorship, feedback routes and power. Ask about practical support needs without requiring a diagnosis or private personal history.
4. Choose the response: clarification, support, repair conversation, mediation, escalation, performance plan, or institutional route.
5. For an agreed work change, record the expectation, feasible support and review point. Keep proposals distinct from agreements. Share a summary only with appropriate recipients; it may be unsafe to send it to someone implicated in a threat or retaliation concern.

## Decision rules

- If the expectation was not explicit, own the ambiguity before giving corrective feedback.
- If safety, harassment, retaliation, coercion, serious career-risk harm or repeated failed repair appears, offer an appropriate independent or institutional route. One serious appropriation or threat need not await repetition.
- If performance is the concern, compare observed work with communicated expectations and check training, resources, workload, reported constraints and project fit. Repeated missed commitments are observations; low motivation or misconduct are not conclusions to infer from them. Route substantiated policy concerns through the responsible process.
- If criticism is needed, keep it claim-focused, evidence-based, assumption-aware, and actionable.
- If a project failed, classify the failure before deciding what to do next.

## Variants and edge cases

- Unclear expectations: reset norms and write them down.
- Harsh criticism: distinguish intellectual discomfort from personal attack.
- Performance plan: use only after expectations, support, evidence, timeline, consequences, and institutional requirements are clear.
- Distress: stay non-clinical and connect to qualified resources.
- Authorship conflict: return to documented contribution criteria and escalation route.

For a credit dispute, identify whether a contribution is missing, eligibility or order is disputed, a policy violation is alleged, or someone objects to publication under their name. Reconstruct the actual work and changes, then use the applicable criteria and collective decision process. An old agreement is revisable, and a historical supervisor-decision convention does not create a permission requirement for independent advice. An editor normally lacks evidence to adjudicate eligibility; use the responsible institution's process when unresolved. Withdrawal trades credit against responsibility, and correction is not guaranteed. Read [contributions, authorship and order](lab-leadership.md#contributions-authorship-and-order) for the separate description, eligibility and byline decisions.

The performance tree is a question aid, not a diagnosis. A disagreement that improvement is needed does not establish which person is wrong. Ability and motivation can interact with resources, supervision, workload, expectations and incentives. Use the fuller account before attributing lack of effort. A formal plan may be appropriate after ordinary feedback and support, but only within the responsible institution's process; poor fit here does not establish lack of potential elsewhere.

## Anti-patterns

- Pretending expectations were clear when they were not.
- Starting with character judgments.
- Using a formal plan as the first clear expectation.
- Calling humiliation rigor.
- Treating every failed experiment as wasted work.
- Telling a vulnerable person to confront a powerful person alone.

## Diagnostic questions

- What was the exact expectation, and how was it communicated?
- What evidence shows the person understood it?
- What support, training, resource, or authority was missing?
- What power asymmetry affects what can be said safely?
- What institutional process constrains the response?
- What will happen if this is handled only as a personality conflict?

## Output patterns / mini-templates

Unclear-expectation feedback:

"I realize we have not made the deadline standard explicit. Let's reset it together. The pattern I have seen is ____. What constraints or misunderstandings should I know about? Going forward, the expectation is ____. I will provide ____. Let's check back on ____."

Work-concern review; select relevant questions rather than assigning a cause from this list:

- Expectation clarity.
- Training and resources.
- Workload, access and voluntarily shared practical constraints; no required health disclosure.
- Project fit.
- Conflict or power issue.
- Specific missed commitments and the person's account, without inferred motive.
- Evidence of a policy concern and the responsible route, without a misconduct finding.
- Support offered.
- Timeline and evidence.
- Institutional route.

Questions for an unsuccessful experiment; more than one may apply:

- Did execution, missing controls or a failed control prevent the intended inference?
- Did the design or measurement leave competing explanations unresolved, even if the procedure ran correctly?
- Is the result credible evidence against the prediction under the tested conditions?
- What can be concluded now, and would repetition, redesign or another approach resolve the remaining uncertainty?

A desired finding is not a performance obligation. Controlled nondetection depends on assay capability and the tested conditions; it cannot establish universal absence. A different method can challenge a shared artifact, but there is no mandatory method count. A cropped panel cannot establish what a complete study did or prove misconduct. Detailed experimental design belongs with the research-strategy lens.

## Examples

- A trainee missed deadlines but the lab never defined update cadence: reset the communication norm, document the cadence, then follow up.
- A lab meeting critique hurt: examine its scientific claim and its delivery separately; retain valid criticism while addressing personal or humiliating treatment.
- An experiment "failed": decide whether to repeat, redesign, pivot, publish as negative evidence, archive, or stop.

## When not to apply this

Do not handle legal, employment, harassment, discrimination, visa, clinical, or emergency matters as ordinary feedback. Use qualified routes and keep any script within role limits.

## Scientific Disagreement Vs Interpersonal Conflict

Treat disagreement as useful when the process is safe and claim-focused.

- Set meeting norms before hard disagreement: criticize ideas, not people; make room for quiet members; and state how decisions will be made.
- Use SBIF for feedback: situation, behavior, impact, future.
- Listen for interests and constraints before proposing a fix.
- Remember that no argument does not equal agreement; check for silence, hidden factions, and post-meeting disagreement.
- Use pre-agreed conflict routes for authorship, data, contribution, and role disputes.

An intent explanation can change understanding without cancelling impact. Thanking or clarifying feedback does not require accepting a false claim. Avoidance, direct decision, compromise, dialogue and negotiation can each fit different stakes; a joint solution presumes enough safety and compatible interests. Private feedback about a person's performance does not mean scientific objections need the leader's private approval before a group can hear them.

Serious conduct concerns require behavioral evidence and realistic support. Typologies and unvalidated ratings do not diagnose a person or allow good scores elsewhere to cancel a serious act. Keep a chronology through safe, permitted channels, separating observations from interpretation. A summary email may help an ordinary agreement but may expose someone under threat. Reporting readiness, independent advice, supported discussion, a change of supervision or exit are choices with real costs; do not require confrontation, immediate departure or full disclosure. Funding, references, unfinished papers and residence arrangements can sustain dependence after a move. Establish a channel's actual remit and reporting limits before identifying people, and never guarantee that information will stay private.

For research integrity, use repeated relevant cases and reasoning practice alongside reviewable records and examination of incentives. Pressure, rationalization, authority and poor systems can interact. Preserve relevant evidence and distinguish honest error from inferred intent; the appropriate process decides alleged violations. Training attendance, a completed checklist or a local integrity program does not prove validity or prevent misconduct.

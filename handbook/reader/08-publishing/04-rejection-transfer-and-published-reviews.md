# Rejection, transfer and published reviews

Start with the editor’s reasons for rejection and discuss them with coauthors. If the paper does not suit the journal’s audience, another destination may be appropriate. If the main conclusion lacks support, address that problem before submitting elsewhere.

<a id="resubmission-decision"></a>

## Understanding the rejection

Read the decision letter alongside the reports. Identify the concerns the editor considers decisive: the journal’s audience, the contribution, a weakness in the evidence, or some combination. Reviewers can disagree, and the editor may give one concern more weight than several favorable assessments. [[1]](#ref-resubmission-editors2019).

Discuss the next step with your coauthors before writing to the journal. Separate work that would improve the paper wherever it is submitted from work intended to meet this journal’s expectations. Consider the time required, available resources and whether the revised contribution would justify another attempt there. Agree on an alternative destination as well. [[2, pp. 126–127]](#ref-resubmission-gosling2022).

A rejection with an invitation to submit new work differs from a revision request: the journal may want to reconsider the project only after substantial changes. If the wording leaves this unclear, ask whether the proposed work would make a new submission appropriate and which concern it must resolve. Briefly describe the work rather than asking whether there is any chance of acceptance. [[3]](#ref-resubmission-chat2021); [[4]](#ref-resubmission-wilke2017).

<a id="resubmission-appeal"></a>

## Decide whether to appeal

An appeal is most useful when you can show why the reasoning behind the rejection should change. Correcting an incidental error is unlikely to help if another unresolved concern still justifies rejection. A successful appeal may lead to further revision or review. [[4]](#ref-resubmission-wilke2017); [[1]](#ref-resubmission-editors2019).

Check the journal’s permitted grounds, deadline, recipient and required documents. In the PLOS One process consulted for this chapter, an appeal must be written and sent through the stated route with the appeal form. A significant factual error or documented competing interest is considered only when correcting it would change the decision. Informal appeals are not accepted; while an appeal is active, the manuscript remains under consideration and must not be submitted elsewhere. Other journals may use different procedures. Recheck the applicable instructions before acting. [[5]](#ref-resubmission-plosprocess).

<a id="resubmission-letter"></a>

### Explain the decisive issue

Open with the manuscript title or identifier and the request for reconsideration. State the editor’s decisive concern, then explain the error or new information with precise manuscript locations. Where several concerns determined the decision, address each; do not select only the easiest criticism. Supply a point-by-point response if the journal requires one. [[4]](#ref-resubmission-wilke2017).

New results may change an editor’s assessment if they resolve the reason for rejection. State what the completed experiments show and identify any work still proposed. If the concern is relevance to the journal, explain what its readers would learn that the editor’s assessment overlooked. [[1]](#ref-resubmission-editors2019).

<a id="resubmission-appeal-example"></a>**Example: appealing a factual misunderstanding**

In the fictional Brake study, inspired by Tang and colleagues (2024) [[6]](#ref-resubmission-tang2024), disrupting translation leaves the RNA-derived DNA product intact but removes growth arrest and phage restriction. Separate expression of the Brake protein restores both effects. Suppose the editor rejects the paper on the mistaken premise that the translation-disrupting change also prevented DNA production, so the protein’s contribution could not be distinguished from the DNA’s. The appeal can point to the control already present in Figure 3A.

Dear Editor,

We would like to appeal the decision on our manuscript, *An RNA-templated gene arrests bacterial growth and restricts phage propagation*.

The decision rests on the concern that our translation-disrupting change also abolishes DNA production. Figure 3A shows that the DNA product is retained in this construct. Growth arrest and phage restriction are lost despite this retained DNA production (Figure 3B), and both are restored by expressing the Brake protein separately (Figure 3C).

These results distinguish the protein’s contribution from DNA production and address the reason given for rejection. Would you reconsider the manuscript in light of this control? We can also revise the Results passage and Figure 3 legend to make the comparison more prominent.

Thank you for considering our appeal.

If the editor had also rejected the paper for insufficient novelty, the appeal would need to address that judgment too.

<a id="resubmission-permission"></a>

### Resubmitting to the same journal

If the reports raise a possible conflict of interest or inappropriate conduct, describe the specific evidence through the journal’s complaints or appeal procedure. Explain separately whether it affects the scientific decision. PLOS One, for example, asks authors to raise concerns about correspondence with journal staff and sets explicit grounds for an appeal. [[5]](#ref-resubmission-plosprocess).

A substantially revised manuscript may be eligible for a fresh submission to the same journal, but check its rules or obtain the editor’s agreement first. Disclose the previous submission and explain the changes. Wilke advocates this route as an alternative to appeal; its suitability depends on the journal and the extent of revision. [[4]](#ref-resubmission-wilke2017).

If you abandon a pending appeal, notify the journal and resolve its status before submitting elsewhere. After a final rejection, return to the scientific concerns and the next journal’s criteria with your coauthors. Keep an appeal and a transfer or new submission as separate routes; do not pursue them at the same time. [[5]](#ref-resubmission-plosprocess); [[7]](#ref-resubmission-naturetransfer); [[2, pp. 126–127]](#ref-resubmission-gosling2022).

<a id="resubmission-transfer"></a>

## Transfer or submit elsewhere

A transfer offer is worth considering alongside other suitable journals. Check the destination’s audience, publication criteria, article type, charges and licensing arrangements using the approach in [Choosing a journal and submitting a paper](01-choosing-a-journal-and-submitting-a-paper.md#submission-chapter). Agree the choice with your coauthors. [[2, pp. 126–127]](#ref-resubmission-gosling2022).

Find out whether the offer is an automated suggestion, a recommendation from the original editor, or follows consultation with the receiving editor. Nature Portfolio describes all three; the receiving journal makes its own editorial decision, and authors choose whether to transfer. [[7]](#ref-resubmission-naturetransfer).

Check which files and correspondence will transfer, including earlier versions, and remove confidential material you do not want to send. The Nature Portfolio guide consulted for this chapter permits file changes during transfer. It also prohibits an appeal while the paper is being transferred for consideration elsewhere. Check the receiving journal’s applicable instructions before proceeding. [[7]](#ref-resubmission-naturetransfer).

Review transfer follows the journals’ agreements about confidentiality and identity. Do not forward an editor’s confidential material or reveal a reviewer on the assumption that permission to use the report also permits disclosure. COPE advises journals and reviewers to establish what can be shared in a transfer. [[8, p. 6]](#ref-resubmission-cope2017).

<a id="resubmission-package"></a>

### Prepare the next submission

Correct known errors and unsupported claims in the next version. For a new submission, address valid criticisms and decide whether requests for a broader study are necessary for the new destination. If a claim depends on missing evidence, obtain it or revise the claim throughout the paper. [[2, pp. 126–127]](#ref-resubmission-gosling2022).

Some transfer routes allow a plan for outstanding work. Nature Portfolio advises distinguishing completed changes from proposed work and explaining the effort required. Establish the receiving journal’s interest before undertaking substantial destination-specific work. [[7]](#ref-resubmission-naturetransfer). Detailed response planning is covered in [Responding to peer review](03-responding-to-peer-review.md#pilot).

Update the cover letter and replace obsolete files. With an ordinary submission, previous reports may not reach the receiving editor automatically; follow its instructions on submission history and earlier correspondence. For transfers, check that the intended response and revised files accompany the manuscript. [[7]](#ref-resubmission-naturetransfer); [[2, pp. 126–127]](#ref-resubmission-gosling2022).

<a id="resubmission-history"></a>

## Read and publish review histories

Published reviews can show how a paper’s evidence and interpretation were challenged and revised. Begin with the final article, then follow an important concern through the report, the authors’ response and the corresponding change. Note the dates, manuscript versions and journals involved. A report written for an earlier version may criticize a claim that no longer appears in the paper; a request from one journal may reflect its particular expectations. [[9]](#ref-resubmission-cheeseman2018).

<a id="resubmission-reading"></a>

### Follow the concern into the paper

For each concern that matters to your reading, identify what the reviewer questioned and how the authors answered. Did they add evidence, correct an analysis, explain an existing control, narrow a claim or leave the point unresolved? Check the final paper rather than relying on the response’s description of the change. If the corresponding manuscript version is unavailable, you may be able to assess the final claim without reconstructing exactly what the reviewer saw. [[9]](#ref-resubmission-cheeseman2018).

<a id="resubmission-history-example"></a>**Example: following a corrected claim**

In this Brake example, the recorded experiment measured growth arrest; recovery was not tested.

- *The growth trajectories in Figure 4B do not establish survival. Did the arrested cells resume growth after infection?*

We did not measure recovery after infection. We have removed the survival claim and now describe the observed growth arrest. The Results passage accompanying Figure 4B now states: “Infected cells stopped growing during observation; whether they subsequently recovered was not tested.”

**Final Results passage**

Infected cells stopped growing during observation; whether they subsequently recovered was not tested.

The claim is corrected; recovery remains untested.

When using published correspondence to learn about reviewing, examine how the reviewer explains a problem and whether the response resolves it in the manuscript. The number of review rounds and the severity of the language do not tell you whether a criticism was justified or a repair adequate. [[10]](#ref-resubmission-plostoolbox); [[9]](#ref-resubmission-cheeseman2018).

<a id="resubmission-consent"></a>

### Check what becomes public

“Open review” can describe different arrangements. Reports may be published without reviewer names; a signed report may be visible to authors without being publicly available. Review transfer and post-publication commenting are further, separate practices. Check the journal’s model rather than inferring these choices from the label. [[10]](#ref-resubmission-plostoolbox).

Before agreeing to publish a review history, inspect what it includes and who can consent to publication. In the PLOS One process consulted for this chapter, authors can opt into publication of decision letters, reports, responses and attachments; names appear when reviewers have signed their reports, and ethical considerations can limit publication. Recheck the journal’s model and permissions for the history you intend to publish. [[5]](#ref-resubmission-plosprocess).

If earlier reports came from another journal, ask how their publication is authorized and how the original context will be identified. Keep confidential correspondence private until the applicable permissions are clear. For teaching or quoting from an already published report, check its stated license and cite the particular report and version. [[8, pp. 5–6]](#ref-resubmission-cope2017); [[9]](#ref-resubmission-cheeseman2018).

<a id="resubmission-checklist"></a>**Before taking the next step**

- Coauthors agree on the route and the work required before proceeding.

- An appeal addresses a decision-relevant issue and meets the journal’s instructions.

- The paper is no longer under consideration elsewhere when the next submission begins.

- The next package addresses valid criticisms and contains the intended files and correspondence.

- Any shared or published reviews follow the relevant permissions and identify their manuscript context.

# CVs, resumes, letters and references

Read the advertisement to establish what the employer needs and which documents, format and deadline it requires. Choose examples that show how your experience meets the selection criteria. Use the requested letter or statement to explain connections that a reader may not see from a list of appointments and achievements.

<a id="applications-choose"></a>

## Choose the document

An academic CV presents education, research, publications, teaching, awards and service. A resume selects the experience and capabilities most relevant to a particular role. Document names and conventions vary between countries: use the employer’s instructions to decide how much detail to include. [[4]](#ref-applications-kelsky2016); [[1]](#ref-applications-gsocv).

Keep one private master record of roles, dates, contributions, outputs and collaborators. When preparing an application, select evidence from that record against the advertised requirements. A short working table can help you decide what to include:

Keep factual labels precise: submitted, under review, preprint and published do not mean the same thing. A stable PDF is sensible when the call permits it and layout matters; check the submitted file after export. [[4]](#ref-applications-kelsky2016).

<a id="applications-cv-resume"></a>

## Organize the CV or resume

Decide the section order before polishing individual entries. In an academic CV, research appointments and publications may lead an application for a postdoctoral position; teaching experience deserves more space in an application for a teaching position. Use clear section names and include grants, mentoring and service where relevant. Within dated sections, list recent work first and use consistent dates, role titles and publication details. [[3]](#ref-applications-harvardocs2016); [[1]](#ref-applications-gsocv).

In a faculty application, dates, project names and descriptions of your contribution must agree across the CV, letter and statements. The documents develop different aspects of the application: the CV records the work, the letter explains how your experience fits the position, and the research statement develops the proposed program. [[7]](#ref-applications-snapp2023).

The examples follow Mira Okafor, a fictional postdoc applying for a research program-manager role involving collaborations between genomic researchers and clinical teams.

These are working outlines, not universal templates. If the advertisement emphasizes teaching, regulation or a particular method, the order and space should change accordingly.

For a resume, choose the few experiences that answer the role’s questions. Explain the research in terms the intended reader can understand while retaining the technical detail needed to judge your work. Name the problem or responsibility, the action you personally took, and the output a new reader can assess. Use the vocabulary of the call when it accurately describes your work. [[6]](#ref-applications-nsecvresume); [[1]](#ref-applications-gsocv).

Use short entries, visible dates and enough white space to separate sections. Quantify the scale or outcome when it helps—for example, the number of people trained or projects coordinated. Keep method names that establish a technical qualification; explain their purpose when the first reader may be a recruiter outside your field. [[6]](#ref-applications-nsecvresume).

<a id="applications-entry-example"></a>**Example: one factual contribution, two document entries**

**CV — Research experience**

**Postdoctoral researcher, Systems Genomics Group, 2024–present**
 Cross-laboratory perturbation screen

- Developed and documented the reproducible analysis workflow.

- Coordinated a results review with clinical and computational collaborators.

- Trained two junior researchers to run and check the workflow.

**Resume — Project coordination**

**Postdoctoral researcher, Systems Genomics Group, 2024–present**

- Coordinated a perturbation-screen project across two laboratories, using one documented analysis workflow for the joint review.

- Presented the findings and unresolved questions to clinical and computational collaborators, then recorded the checks assigned to each laboratory.

- Trained two junior researchers to run and check the workflow.

The resume entry gives more space to coordination and shared analysis because these are central to the target role. For a computational-research position, the analysis itself would need more technical detail.

<a id="applications-letter"></a>

## Writing a cover letter

When a letter is requested, name the role, explain your interest in the organization and develop a few examples of relevant experience. Use each paragraph to address a requirement, with enough context to explain your contribution. Leave the full chronology to the CV or resume. [[2]](#ref-applications-gsoletter).

For this example, the advertised role includes coordinating joint projects and organizing research reviews.

<a id="applications-letter-example"></a>**Example: a short letter that connects fit to evidence**

Dear Hiring Committee,

I am applying for the Research Program Manager position because your joint-project program brings genomic researchers and clinical teams together around shared studies. Coordinating that kind of work has become the part of my postdoctoral research that I most want to develop.

During my postdoc, I coordinated a perturbation screen across two laboratories. I developed the shared analysis workflow and trained two junior researchers to run and check it. This gave both teams a common set of results to discuss while retaining access to the underlying analysis.

I also organized the project review with clinical and computational colleagues. I presented the findings and unresolved questions, then documented the checks each laboratory would undertake. This combination of technical understanding, a common review process and clear follow-up is what I would bring to the institute’s joint projects.

Thank you for considering my application. I would be pleased to discuss the position with you.

Sincerely,

Mira Okafor

The letter connects Mira’s interest in the institute’s joint projects to her experience coordinating analysis and agreeing follow-up work with collaborators. The resume lists these contributions; the letter explains why she wants to make this work central to her next role.

<a id="applications-references"></a>

## Asking for references

Choose referees who know your work well enough to describe your contribution. Ask whether they can provide a strong reference, leaving time to approach someone else if necessary. Discuss why you are seeking this role and which parts of your work together are relevant to it. Send the position description, current application materials, those selected examples, and the submission instructions and deadline. For several applications, keep the deadlines and links in one table. [[5]](#ref-applications-martin2017).

If a referee asks for a draft or notes, provide factual material: the context, your role, the result and relevant examples from your work together. The referee must decide what to endorse and write the final assessment. Check submission confirmations and send a polite reminder when a deadline approaches.

Before submitting, compare the documents with the call. Check dates, role titles and publication status across the packet; ask a reader to identify your strongest qualifications from the first page; and inspect the exported files for layout problems.
